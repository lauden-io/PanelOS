# Panel app (`app/`)

Native Rust app with the Slint UI toolkit. It draws its own interface on the panel's GPU and reads the touchscreen itself, without a compositor or window system; a web view comes in only in dashboard mode.

## Interface

- **Rendering**: Slint 1.18 with its Skia renderer on OpenGL ES (Mesa Panfrost on the Mali-G31, libraries from the web bundle), drawn into a GBM surface whose buffers go to the screen with page flips (`src/gpu.rs`; the GBM / EGL glue follows Slint's KMS backend). The app runs its own event loop (`src/platform.rs`): page flips, touch, wake-ups from other threads, timers. It renders the next frame while the last one waits for its flip, and only when something changed or an animation runs.
- **Pages**: the pages sit side by side in a pager and are swiped with the finger (1:1, 260 ms to settle). The swipe has no end: after the last page the first comes in from the same side (only the page dots jump back), and the other way round; a single page does not move. The list, the order, each page's look and what it shows come from the PanelOS integration (`pages` in its options); without it: home, weather, music, timers. Kinds: home (the weather page), weather, clock, music, timers, thermostat, quick controls, cameras, calendar, to-do, notifications. Every kind may be on the list any number of times, each page with its own settings and data (see *Pages of one kind* below): ten music pages each show their own player, two quick controls pages their own tiles, every weather page its own background and widgets. The weather details page is such a page too: a background (or the first custom page's) and weather widgets on the same 4 × 4 grid (by default the weather now, the details, the hourly and the daily forecast), whose widgets open nothing; the panel's own edit mode offers it the weather widgets only. A home widget opens its page; a page the list lacks slides in next to the current one without a dot and goes once left.
- **Pages of one kind** (`PageSlot.n`, `PageSlot.cfg`; `pages::kind_pages`, `pages::acting_slot`, `pages::with_inst`): each slot of the pager knows which page of its kind it is (`n`, 0 for the first) and its place in the integration's list (`cfg`, -1 for a temporary page or the panel's own list). What a page shows of its own lives in a per-page row of its kind (`quick-inst`, `thermo-inst`, `cam-inst`, `todo-inst`, `cal-inst`, `music-inst`, `timers-inst`, `clock-inst`, `info-inst`, `home-inst`: one Slint struct per page, rows in the pages' order), and each page binds `root.<kind>-inst[slot.n]`; values that change every second have rows of their own (`music-pos`, `home-music-pos`), so a running time never touches the rest of its page. Each kind's refresh makes every page's row from that page's own entities and options (`pages::inst_pages`; a kind without a page keeps one row of defaults, which a temporary page shows) and sets a row only when it changed, so a state that one page shows refreshes that page alone, and the neighbour seen during a swipe already shows its own content (the cameras fetch frames only for the cameras page on screen; the others keep their last ones). Data is fetched per entity and shared: every calendar and to-do list a page shows is read, every player a page or a music widget shows has its own cover (`Art`, fetched per player). A page's menus (its pickers, settings, editors, keyboards) keep one state per kind, shown only by the page on screen (`… && root.page == i`, or the page's own `on-screen`); they close when the page leaves the screen. Taps act on the page they come from: `acting(kind)` is the page on screen when it is of that kind, else the first of the kind, and `page_entities`, `page_option`, `page_look` and `page_set` all go through it, so a look, an option or a player set on the panel goes to that page (`panelos/page_set` with its `cfg`), and the panel's copy of the settings takes it at once. Until the integration's settings carry it (at most 10 s), such a change holds over older copies of the settings that come back meanwhile (`keep_page_sets`: a copy sent before the integration had it, or one a new Home Assistant connection got first), and a new connection asked for meanwhile waits up to 2 s for the integration's answer to it, so a player picked in "Play on" shows at once and stays. A request that names a kind (a ring, a shown view, the bell, the status bar's marks, the dev input's `act`) goes to the page of that kind on screen, else the first one. One page sets what is the panel's alone: the synthwave scene, its cars and traffic, the panel's weather (the first weather details page that names one, else the integration's weather entity) and the weather details' background default (the first custom page's); the panel's player (music widgets without their own player, voice music commands, the satellite's report) is the player of the music page shown last; the notification window takes the first notifications page's look. Timers, alarms, the stopwatch, the notifications and the clock are the panel's: every page of those kinds shows the same ones, each in its own look and options (a timers page its own functions, tab, colours, stopwatch and alarm looks and synthwave scene; a clock page its own parts, letters and colour; a notifications page its own pager finish).
- **Over every page**: the status bar, the timer island, the page dots, the quick shade (a swipe down that starts on the status bar; it follows the finger), the voice view, new notifications (the window, or a strip under the status bar, as set in the integration), status screens and critical alerts, the screensaver.
- **Status bar**: every part has its fixed place on every page. At the left the hint (the connection problem first, then the muted microphone's line, then the wake word), in the middle one group of marks, always centred and always in this order: microphone off, speaker off, timers, the bell (with the count; with do not disturb the bell sleeps, also without notifications). A mark comes and goes with a small jelly; a tap on it turns the microphone or the speaker back on (the speaker at the level it had before), opens the timers page or the notifications. A page's own chip at the right is 152 px wide, a longer name cut.
- **Timer island**: the running timers in one capsule under the status bar, the nearest first, each with a ring in its colour (red in its last minute, pause bars when paused), then "+N". A tap on a timer opens the timers page with that timer (its menu in the plain and synthwave looks when two or more run), "+N" the page. A flick up flies it into the status bar's timer mark; it comes back by itself when a new timer starts or one enters its last minute. It stays under the shade and the voice view and is not shown on the timers page.
- **New notifications**: a window in the middle under the status bar, over a dimmed page, in the notifications page's look (a card, a sticky note, a pager, a TV news flash). Dismiss removes the notification (in Home Assistant too), OK closes the window and marks it read, a tap beside it works as OK. Several come into one window ("1 of 3"): a sideways swipe or the pager's arrows step, OK steps to the next. It stays for the integration's seconds (a finger on it holds the time; at the end it closes and the notification stays unread), or, with "until answered", until OK or Dismiss. In do not disturb, on the screensaver or under an alert strip only the bell's count changes.
- **Status screens and critical alerts**: the integration watches the house-wide rules and tells the panels. A status screen (a room in use, someone on a call …) covers the panel while its state lasts, with the panel's own words for its category unless the integration gives its own; a swipe or its button folds it into a strip in its colour right under the status bar (the bar stays visible) on every page: a tap on the strip opens the screen again, its × closes the strip (the state goes on without it; the timer island moves down under the strip, and the strip steps aside while a page's menu is open); one with a countdown ends by itself. A critical alert (smoke, CO, gas, water) takes the whole screen in plain red on the notifications page, whatever its look, and sounds until it is held for two seconds (when the integration allows holding); it then stays in a red strip, without ×, until its sensor clears. While they are active, the notifications page lists them above its notifications (see below), also a status screen whose strip was closed.
- **Notifications page**: the list, unread first, in one of four looks (plain, fridge notes, a pager, TV news). Above the notifications stand the active ones of the integration's alerts and status screens, the critical alert first (once held, in red), then the status screens, the newest first, each with its own words, picture and colour: a row in its colour in the plain look, a magnetic sign on the fridge, a "■" line at the top of the pager's list ("!" for the critical alert), a NOW headline in its colour on the TV news (ALERT for the critical alert). A tap on a status screen (on the pager: OK while it is the top line) opens it again, on top of the others, as a tap on its strip does; folded again, it has its strip back. "Clear all" and Dismiss leave them: they go when their state ends. The critical alert's row only shows it. In the plain look a tapped row opens the notification window's card (without its time line) over a dimmed page; a button or a tap beside it closes it. The other looks open one in their own view (the note to the front, the pager's LCD message, the big story). The settings (from the quick shade's page row) are the look's own menu: a card in the plain look, a magnetic whiteboard on the fridge, the pager's own LCD menu (▲ ▼ move the cursor, OK or a tap on a line changes it; the finish and the time format apply at once, a new look when the menu is left), the TV's on-screen menu. Each has the look and the panel's time format (the pager also its finish, the same value as the integration's); a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's menu. The menu and the open notification close when the page leaves the screen. The pager's and the TV news clock follow the time format.
- **Do not disturb**: from the quick shade, or set in the integration (on, or between two times). Critical alerts always come through; alarms, timers, status screens, the doorbell and voice answers by default (each can be turned off in the integration: alarms and timers then ring silently, a status screen comes as its strip only, and without voice answers the assistant still listens and answers, but without its listening chime and without speaking: the answer only shows on the screen); new notifications go silently into the list. Announcements from Home Assistant play as usual. The stored setting also keeps a `calls` entry, a placeholder that nothing reads and the PanelOS page does not show.
- **Quick shade**: in two steps that scroll as one. The first pull shows brightness with a small Auto box (the light sensor sets the level; moving the slider unticks it), the four tiles as a row of icons and a chevron; pulling on (or the chevron) opens the second step: the title and the PanelOS mark, brightness, volume (set when the finger lifts, with a chime at the new level), the tiles with their names (microphone, theme Auto → Dark → Light, do not disturb, screen off) and, when the page on screen has settings, its page row ("Music settings" over the look's name, with the look's icon): a tap closes the shade and the page opens its settings in its look's menu. Under both: what runs now, a card per timer (+1 min, pause, stop) and the newest notifications (a sideways swipe dismisses one, a tap opens it in the window). At the top of its content a pull down is the next step, at the end a push up the step before, then it closes; every pull starts again at the first step. A tap on the PanelOS mark opens a card, and holding the mark in that card opens the settings list; every row opens its page (the back arrow returns to the list). When the rows do not all fit, the card ends partway into a row, which fades out at its edge until the list is scrolled to its end:
  - **Wi-Fi**: the network list (strongest first, the saved ones marked), its password screen and a hidden network by name. A new network is tried before it is kept, so a wrong password or a network without Home Assistant never locks the panel out (see *Changing the Wi-Fi network* below).
  - **Home Assistant**: the setup screen with the address in use; a new address where Home Assistant answers is saved (the other settings in `app.json` stay) and the app starts again with it (another Home Assistant asks for the pairing again). The same address only says so.
  - **Display**: theme (the shade's three-way switch), brightness with its Auto box, time format (24-hour / 12-hour), screensaver after and style (a clock or the screen off). The same settings as the PanelOS page's Display section: a change on either side shows on the other.
  - **Voice**: the microphone, the wake word and a second one (or none), named as the voice satellite's models name them.
  - **Sound**: the volume (a chime at the new level when the finger lifts).
  - **Storage**: per partition, read on the panel when the page opens: the PanelOS slot from its boot image header, the data partitions with their biggest folders, what is not used yet.
  - **Touch** (the calibration), **About** (version, model, the Panel ID, the panel's and Home Assistant's addresses, the project page and the issue tracker as QR codes: a tap on a link shows its code), **Restart**.
- **Touch calibration** (settings list, or from the integration): nine points to touch; a touch counts only within 40 px of the point shown (a tap elsewhere is not a measurement: the line asks for the point itself). Cancel leaves it at any time without changing anything; started from the settings list it goes back there, also when it is done. The panel corrects the touch positions only when the offsets are systematic (all the same way, more than 3 px on average, spread under 6 px), stores the correction and says what it did. A touch device that disappears (its driver bound again) is let go and looked for again every 5 s, without restarting the app.
- **Music page**: the record on the turntable has the cover's own colour as it is, black, white and grey too (the strongest hue of a 32-pixel copy, weighted by how colourful and common it is; for a grey cover its mean tone). The page's accent (buttons, bars) is the same hue brought to a readable brightness (a grey cover's own tone; the panel's accent without a cover); the tone arm keeps the brand colour. It crossfades with the cover (400 ms). The platter and the cassette reels spin up and coast down with a speed that never jumps: a start while it still coasts, or a stop while it spins up, goes on from the speed it has. Every view shows one player only, its cover included: a player without a cover leaves nothing of the one shown before behind the page. The cassette's volume bar is held first: a press held still for half a second takes it (its thumb grows meanwhile), then it follows the finger left and right and the pages do not turn until the finger lifts; a tap anywhere on it sets that point, and a press that moves at once stays a page swipe. Its keys and volume row sit clear of the page dots. A play or pause from the panel (the play key, the turntable's arm) turns every view of that player at once, and a pause holds the time of that moment until the player reports it (at most 3 s). Every look has shuffle and repeat keys (repeat steps off, all, one; both are out of use while what plays has no length, a stream). The page's settings (the quick shade's page row) hold the look (plain, full cover, turntable, cassette, half cassette), what the record or the tape holds (one track or the whole queue), the wood of the counter under the turntable, a blurred cover (plain) or the cover behind the cassette, and long titles cut off or scrolling at one of five speeds.
- **Quick controls page** (tiles, mixing desk, flight deck): up to eight controls a page (more go on another quick controls page); a tap toggles; a long press on a tile, on a channel's tape under its fader or on a lever's plate opens the control's details in the look's menu (a card grown from the tile, the channel's rack unit above the desk, the switch's overhead sub-panel). They hold what the control can do: on / off, its level with its unit (brightness, position, set temperature), colour temperature, colours, the effect, a cover's open / stop / close (the key of the way it moves lit); a value set stays where it was let go until the device follows (at most 3 s). The page's settings (the quick shade's page row) hold the look; a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's. The tiles look has an All off button: it turns off the page's lights, switches, toggle helpers, fans, media players and climate devices. The desk's tapes are as wide as their strips (8 channels fill the desk, fewer get up to 84 px) and take a long name in smaller letters or in two lines, the deck's plates the same. The flight deck paints group frames over its levers: automatic (neighbours of one kind: lights, shutters, climate, switches, media …), off (one frame over the top row with the panel's area) or the user's own groups; a frame stops at the end of a row and the next row starts a new one with the same name. A colour light's lamp lights in the light's own colour at the throw: its last colour is kept, also over a restart (`quick-colours.json` beside the settings); a light the panel has never seen on lights when the light reports back.
- **Clock faces** (the screensaver, the clock page and the clock widget, `panels.slint`, `home.slint`, `clocks.slint`): digital, big digits, round, square, minimal, numerals, wandering, split-flap (the widget: the time in words instead of wandering), and three drawn ones: nixie tubes on a walnut plinth (smoky glass, so a background shows through; seconds optional), an LCD quartz watch (its resin case from 3 × 2 cells, the LCD alone below that; AL when an alarm is on) and a red twin-bell clock (its alarm hand at the next alarm). The drawn ones keep their own proportions and are scaled to fit, centred. Smallest widget sizes: nixie 3 × 1 (4 × 1 with seconds), LCD 2 × 1, twin-bell 2 × 2.
- **Timers tab** (`timers.slint`, its menus in `timer_menus.slint`, the arcade screen in `arcade.slint`): five looks, plain, kitchen, hourglass, synthwave and flip. The plain and synthwave looks lay three or more timers out in a grid of four cells: with three the fourth is a "+", with more the grid shows the four that end soonest (running ones by the time left, then paused ones), each cell keeping its place while its set stays. Kitchen, hourglass and flip show one timer big with a chip for every timer (equally wide, wrapping onto more rows) and the round + whenever timers run; plain and synthwave show the round + with one or two timers. In the plain and synthwave looks, with two or more timers, a tap on a timer opens its menu over it (the name, a ring with the time counting down, +1 min, pause / go on, cancel); the "+" and the quick starts' custom button open the new timer (hour and minute drums that follow the finger and glide one row per step, on the flip look split-flap cards: a tap on a card's lower half adds one, on its upper half takes one; a label line; Start); the quick shade's page row opens the page's settings (functions, colours, the timer look and, for synthwave, its scene). Each menu is an object of the look (a card, a recipe card on the fridge, a parchment scroll, an arcade screen, a split-flap board); on the arcade screen every choice is a block and moves in whole 40 ms frames. A new look closes the menu and opens it again in the new look; menus close when the page leaves the screen, the screensaver comes or something rings. Whenever the tab comes into view, every timer runs from zero to where it stands in its look's way; out of view the timers wait at zero. A timer's time rolls its changed digits as it counts down. Labels take up to 30 characters (alarms 32): the keyboard view counts them and shakes the field when a key is pressed at the limit, and the end of a long label stays in view. A timer's name that comes from Home Assistant (a timer started by voice, or by another device) is cut to 30 characters the same way (`pages::timer_label`).
- **Stopwatch and alarm looks** (timers page options, besides the timers' own look): a chronograph, a timing tower, an LCD sports watch and nixie tubes for the stopwatch; a twin-bell clock, a sunrise sky, a clock radio and a 24-hour dial for the alarms. The tabs, the page dots and the buttons stay where the plain look has them.
  - **Alarms in every look**: the + (top right) opens a new alarm, except on the clock radio, whose three preset keys are the alarms (AL1-AL3 lit while on; an empty key is a "+" for a new alarm; the radio handles three). The plain look lists the alarms with switches (a tap on a row edits it); the twin-bell, sunrise and 24-hour dial show them as chips, two to a row, further rows scroll. A tap on a chip or a preset key switches that alarm on or off, a hold of half a second opens it in the editor (on release). The radio sits in the middle of the free space under the tabs and has a 12H / 24H slide switch on its body.
  - **The alarm editor** comes down under the status bar as an object of the alarm look (`alarm_menus.slint`): the brand card, the twin-bell's enamel face, the dawn sky, the clock radio's plastic with its LED and HOUR / MIN keys and day keys with LEDs, the night dial with a 24-hour ruler whose sleep arc follows the drums. Time (drums that glide a row in 180 ms; in 12-hour mode hours 1-12 and an AM / PM drum), days, sound (a designed sound plays once as it is picked), label (the keyboard), Save, and Delete for a saved alarm. A tap beside it closes it without saving.
  - **Each alarm look rings its own way** (`AlarmRing` in `alarm_menus.slint`); the plain look keeps the brand ringing screen. Snooze 9 min sits in the same place in every look. A snoozed alarm shows in the next-alarm line of every look ("Snoozed · rings at 6:54") until it rings again, and its row, chip or key shows it on meanwhile (also a once alarm, which switches itself off as it rings). Switching it off there, deleting it, saving it with another time, days or name, or switching it off or deleting it from Home Assistant (the PanelOS page, a voice assistant's alarm tools) ends the snooze too: it does not ring again and the line goes.
  - **Time format**: every alarm time (rows, chips, keys, the next-alarm lines, the editor, the ringing screens) follows the panel's 24-hour / 12-hour value (`Clock.h24`); the analog faces show no option.
  - **Laps** (the flag while it runs): every stopwatch look lists them, newest on top, older ones scroll into view: plain three rows, the chronograph moves its dial left and lists them beside it, the timing tower five rows, the LCD two LCD lines, the nixie two lines.
  - **Entrances**: whenever the alarm or the stopwatch tab comes into view (the pages settle on the timers page with it open, it is picked, the screensaver or a ring ends), its look runs once from its zero to where it stands, in at most 0.7 s for the alarms and 0.65 s for the stopwatch (whose time then goes on live); out of view it waits at its zero, and data updates do not start it again.
  - **Settings** of the alarm and stopwatch tabs come from the quick shade's page row ("Timers settings · Alarms · Clock radio") in the look's own menu (`alarm_menus.slint`, `watch_menus.slint`): functions, colours, the time format (alarm looks with digits) and the look; the stopwatch's in the brand card, the steel case back, the timing board, the LCD watch and a walnut base with a brass plate. A look change reopens the menu as the new look's. The menus and the editor close when the page leaves the screen, the tab changes or a ring starts.
- **Each look moves in its own way**: besides the shared motions (menus, digits, marks), every look has motions of its own. They are drawn only while they run.
- **Synthwave scenes** (home background, timers, thermostat): mountains, city, beach and space (its light changing from day to night). On the home, weather and thermostat pages each scene has a day, a night and a dawn view, chosen by the sun: day above +3°, night below −6°, a fade between; in the morning the dawn view comes in over the night view while the sun rises from −12° to −6° and then fades into the day with it (never in the evening). The floor colour mixes the same way. While a scene is on screen a new sun value glides in over 3 s in whole 40 ms frames (the sun's values come minutes apart), the view's colour in three steps (each new colour draws the widgets' glow again); out of sight it is taken at once. A scene's dawn pictures are loaded with its day and night ones and drawn only while the dawn shows, so the rest of the day they cost no frame time. The cars drive along the nine middle floor lines (−4 … +4; the outer ones come in from a lower corner). The panel shows one scene on its home, weather and thermostat pages: the first page with a synthwave background of its own chooses it (without one, the first custom page), and the panel's scene menus (the background's palette, the thermostat's neon look) set it on that page. The timers page has its own scene, or a random one: a new one, never the same twice in a row, is picked while the page is out of sight (a second or two after it went, with no finger on the pages), so the page brings it from its first frame however it is opened (a swipe, the island, the status bar's mark, a ring) and it never changes on screen.
- **Rain on the glass** (`src/glass.rs`, `src/glass_gl.rs`): over the photo backgrounds (the weather pages' photos and the weather details' living photo), while it rains, there are drops on the glass; how many follow the scene's rain (drizzle, rain, pouring; a thunderstorm pours). The drops lie on the glass, not on the page: a swipe to the next photo page keeps them, and they fade out with a page without a photo, under the screensaver, the voice view, the shade, a page's menu or a status screen. The drops are simulated on the CPU and drawn by the GPU after Skia's frame: the frame is copied at half size into a texture, and every drop, bead and streak is one small instanced quad whose shader looks through it at that copy (a streak only darkens what is under it). A dry glass, or one off screen, draws nothing and asks for no frame. Both are switches of the panel's device in Home Assistant, on by default: **Rain on the glass**, and **Lightning** for the storm scenes' flashes and bolts.
- **Notifications** keep the time they first came: Home Assistant makes some again with a new time at every start (an invalid config), so the panel remembers each one by its title and text (`notes-seen.json` beside the settings, forgotten a day after it went).
- **Editing a custom page on the panel** (`home_edit.rs`, `HomeGrid` in `home.slint`; only with the integration's "Allow editing on the panel"): the app watches the finger before the interface gets it (`platform.rs` → `home_edit::finger`); a finger that rests 0.7 s on a custom page (or a weather details page) with nothing over it (`home-hold-ok`) lets go of what it pressed (so lifting it taps nothing) and opens the lock; holding the lock fills its ring in 1 s and opens the edit mode. A drag moves a widget (its landing cells outlined), the thick corner resizes it (never below its smallest size), − removes it, a tap opens its settings menu (the widget stays lit in its cell as the live preview, the card unfolds under or over it), + opens "Add widget" (live previews; a pick lights the free cells, a tap on one puts it there) and the palette opens the synthwave background's arcade screen (`arcade.slint`). Each change goes into the panel's copy of the settings (the page shows it at once) and to the integration as the page's widget list (`panelos/page_set` with `items`); a copy of the settings coming back before the change is in them does not undo it. Leaving the page, the screensaver or Done ends the edit mode. A People widget's menu lists every person of Home Assistant as checkboxes, one tap ticks or unticks one person (all ticked: everyone; the last one ticked stays) and keeps the choice as the widget's `people`; under them, **Profile pictures** switches between the people's pictures and their initials.
- **The People widget's heads** (`PeopleW` in `home.slint`, `avatars.rs`): each person's head is their profile picture in Home Assistant (`entity_picture`) or, without one or with the widget's Profile pictures off (opts bit 2, so pictures are the default), their initials in their colour. With "The ones away too" on, everyone not at home is drawn fainter (45 %) inside a grey ring; the ones at home stay in full colour without a ring. The pictures are fetched in a thread of their own with the panel's login (only for Home Assistant's own addresses), decoded (JPEG or PNG), cut to their middle square, shrunk to at most 128 px and made round there (clear outside the circle), so the widget draws them as they are: no clip, no layer, and the faint ones set only their own opacity. They are kept per person and fetched again only when the picture's address changes (one that failed is asked for again after 5 minutes); they are fetched only while a People widget shows pictures (or the widget picker is open), and forgotten otherwise.
- **Menus** (`MenuHost` in `widgets.slint`): every picker and page settings menu comes out of what opened it, as an object of the look (a record sleeve, a J-card, an arcade screen …). The host places it (a picker hangs from the chip, right edge at x 464 from y 48; page settings come down under the status bar, x 16–464), lets it grow to its content up to y 468 (beyond that the look scrolls its body), dims the page to 30 % and closes it on a tap beside it. The look draws the menu and its motion from the host's clocks (`t-in` / `t-out` in ms, `t-in-40` / `t-out-40` for the looks that step in whole 40 ms frames); the clocks run only while a motion lasts, so a menu at rest draws nothing. The shared controls take the look's colours as tokens (`tk-raised`, `tk-line`, `tk-text`, `tk-muted`, `tk-on-text`, `tk-menu`, `tk-pressed`, `tk-track`, `tk-knob`, `tk-accent`; the defaults are the plain look's). The quick shade's page row opens the settings of the page on screen in its look's menu (`PageMenu`).
- **The arcade screen** (`arcade.slint`): the neon looks' menus come up on a small CRT in the scene's colour. Choices on it are arcade blocks: words in the pixel font, the current one an inverse block; what is on it moves in whole 40 ms frames; the screen's own switch-on and switch-off run at 30 frames a second.
- **Thermostat menus** (`thermo_menus.slint`): the device chip opens the rooms, the option pills the options (dial and neon; the remote's keys and the wall's slide switches step them in place), the quick shade's page row the settings. Dial: the brand card. Remote control: a zone LCD in the remote's plastic, the settings on its sliding lid. Wall thermostat: the zone plate with a pilot lamp per zone, the settings behind its hinged front door. Neon: the arcade screen. A tap on a room shows its device; a look picked in the settings closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's. The remote's LCD clock follows the panel's time format; a tap on it switches the format (12H / 24H printed under it). The menus close when the page leaves the screen.
- **Rotary controls**: the thermostat's dial ring (dial and neon looks) and the wall look's knob turn with the finger, relatively, to the device's steps (the wall look's drum counter shows the step the knob is on); the target is sent when the finger lifts, and also when the panel calls the touch off while the control still takes touches (a second finger). A drag that starts on the ring or the knob never turns the page: the page tells the pager where its ring is (`Act.lock-*`), and the pager checks the press against it before its own 100 ms hold ends; a thermostat page takes away only a ring it set itself (`Act.lock-serial`), so a second thermostat page cannot clear the one on screen. A swipe that starts in the middle of the dial or outside the ring turns the page.
- **Thermostat targets** (`thermo.rs`, `Setpoint`): every − / + press (dial, remote, neon) is one step from the value shown, and the page shows it at once. While a change is under way the page shows the panel's value, so Home Assistant's echo of an older value neither moves it nor becomes the base of the next press. A quick row of presses goes out as one call with the value shown, 0.7 s after the last press; a turn goes out when the finger lifts. One call per device at a time: the next value waits until Home Assistant reports the one sent (a device that gets several calls at once may keep only some of them). A value not reported back within 30 s gives way: the page shows Home Assistant's value again. The log has a line for every value sent (`thermostat <entity>: target 19 (3 presses)`) and for every value that did not come back.
- **Calendar views** (`calendar.slint`, `cal.rs`): the plain look's day, 3-day and week views are time grids. Whenever the page comes on screen (a swipe, the screensaver gone) and whenever the view changes, the grid shows the period with today in it (‹ › browse from there: a day, three days or a week at a time; the 3-day view is today and the next two days) and scrolls so the current time is in the middle of the grid, as far as the day allows. An hour is 44 px in the day view; the 3-day and week views fit 08:00–18:00 on their grid. Two fingers moving apart or together vertically make the hours taller or shorter round the hour under the fingers, from the whole day to four hours on the grid; one finger scrolls as before, and the zoom stays until the page comes on screen again or the view changes. Two fingers reach Slint like this: `touch.rs` reads the driver's multitouch slots next to its single-touch emulation; while two contacts are down the pointer stops (the first finger's press is let go with a pointer exit, so it taps and drags nothing), and `platform.rs` sends Slint pinch events (`ScaleRotateGestureHandler` over the grid) once the fingers' vertical spread has changed by 8 px; the finger left after a pinch does nothing until it lifts. An event's description and place are shown readable: HTML tags go (line breaks, paragraphs and list items start lines), entities are read, link targets in angle brackets go, a long bare link shows only its host, lines without letters or digits (separators) go, blank runs become one empty line, and a description ends with "…" after 1500 characters. Every wrapped text in the calendar's menus has `min-width: 0`: a wrapped text's narrowest width is its longest word, and one long link would widen the whole card and push its rows out of sight.
- **Calendar menus** (`cal_menus.slint`): four menus in every look. The chip's picker shows or hides the page's calendars (at least one stays on; the last one shakes; the integration keeps the choice as `hidden_calendars`; the chip reads "4 calendars" or the one name) and, in the plain look, picks the view (kept as the last view, the start view after a restart). The settings (the quick shade's page row) hold the look and the time format. A day's list comes from a month day, a week or 3-day column, a wall calendar day or the tear-off's "+n more"; the departure board's head shows the week's departures. An event's details sit over a list (a tap beside them goes back to it); Edit, for calendars that allow it, turns them into the edit form in the same object: the title on the keyboard, ‹ › a day, all day, and the start and end on the time drums in place (moving the start keeps the length; the end never goes before the start). Save writes the event with `calendar/event/update`; Delete hides it at once and sends `calendar/event/delete` when its undo strip has gone (5 s). Plain is the brand card; the departure board has flap boards, the station's printed timetable and a boarding pass; the tear-off has slips from the pad, its cardboard back and an appointment card; the wall calendar has ruled index cards beside the tapped day. Every calendar time follows the time format (on the departure board's flap cells a 12-hour time reads "6:45P").
- **Time format**: one panel-wide value, 24-hour (the default) or 12-hour, for every clock time; durations never change. It is kept in `settings.json` (`time_24h`) and set in three places, each showing the others' changes: the integration's Time format select (also in the PanelOS page's Display section), the panel's settings → Display, and a look's own 12H / 24H key (`Act` `set.h24` with `"true"` / `"false"`). The UI reads `Clock.h24`; the app writes its clock times with `clock::hm(h, m)` / `clock::local_hm(t)` ("18:45" / "6:45 PM"), an hour alone with `clock::hour_label(h)` ("18" / "6 PM"), and `clock::parse_hm` reads either form back. Digital clock faces show the hour without a leading zero in 12-hour mode and a small AM / PM after the minutes on the digits' baseline (`ClockData.th` and `ampm`; `hh` stays 24-hour for the hands and dials); the split-flap clock a blank first card and an AM / PM flap, the nixie tubes a dark first tube and a small neon PM lamp, the LCD watch its PM segment above the seconds. Analog faces have no option; their texts follow it.
- **To-do page menus** (`todo_menus.slint`): the chip picks the list in the look's own object (the brand card, till paper, the terminal's `todo --lists`, embossed tapes on a dark card, a page of the pad); the page settings (look, time format) open from the quick shade's page row in the same object. The terminal's config applies a new look when its menu is left; elsewhere a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's menu. The label maker's menu tapes come from the app's own tape drawer (`Job::TapeLabel`: a long name goes onto more tapes, the shown list is marked ▶ and rimmed). The shared controls take the look's shape and letters as tokens too (`tk-radius`, `tk-font`, `tk-font-size`, `tk-font-weight`, `tk-caps`; the segmented control's pill `tk-pill`). A new item can take a description and a due date (today, tomorrow or a picked day) in lists that keep them. It lands at the top of its list and opens in 250 ms (lists that can move items: `todo/item/move` without `previous_uid`); a ticked item stays struck through for 1.5 s, then folds away in 250 ms and moves under Completed.
- **Keyboard** (to-do, calendar title, timer and alarm labels, Wi-Fi password and network name): a 0.4 s press on a letter shows its accented forms, the finger slides to one and letting go types it; the globe key switches Latin / Cyrillic letters; an empty field starts with a capital, also after deleting back to the start (the Wi-Fi fields start in small letters).
- **Taps** reach the app through one callback (`Act` in `theme.slint`): what (`music.play`, `quick.toggle`, `set.brightness` …) and a value. Whatever switches, ticks or picks something (checkboxes, switches, segmented controls, dropdown lists, arcade blocks, the rows of pickers and settings, the to-do items, the quick tiles, the alarm switches and chips) takes a tap only: a finger that moved more than 8 px after it went down (the distance after which a list or the pager takes it as a drag) changes nothing, wherever it is let go (`TapArea` in `widgets.slint`). Each page has its own module: `shade.rs`, `quick.rs`, `thermo.rs`, `cam.rs`, `cal.rs`, `todo.rs`, `info.rs`, `mus.rs` (music players), `synth.rs` (the synthwave scenes); clock, weather, home widgets, music and timers in `pages.rs` and `main.rs`.
- **Data**: live states through the Home Assistant connection (`subscribe_entities` for exactly the entities the pages show); calendars and to-do items over Home Assistant's REST API in threads (every 5 min / every minute and after a change); camera frames through the camera proxy only while the cameras page is on screen; notifications through `persistent_notification/subscribe`; the music players for the picker from the PanelOS integration (`/api/panelos/players`), so the panel never loads the whole state list.
- **Smooth by construction**: values that change every second (seconds hands, the music position, the stopwatch) move as UI animations from Slint's animation clock; the app sets a page's data only when it changed and updates rows of a list in place, so nothing is built again or laid out anew while it is on screen; static widget parts are drawn once into layers; pictures arrive as RGBA prepared in the thread that made them. Pictures of the screen (Home Assistant's live preview, screenshots) are read from the buffer on screen through its dma-buf in a thread, never back from the GPU inside a frame. At start, under the start-up picture, and again after the page list changed (under the page shown), every page is drawn once, so the first swipe to it does not have to build it. Text-heavy looks that only change once a minute (the calendar's) are drawn into a layer, so a swipe only moves a picture. The living weather backgrounds (`photo_sky.slint`) draw their still parts once into layers (the drawn themes' backdrop with the sun and the moon; every theme's land with its dusk, dawn and night pictures and the lit city windows) and only what moves over and between them; a theme's land is opaque from a fixed row down in all its pictures, so what lies behind it is drawn above that row only, and a day picture under a night or dusk picture at full opacity is not drawn where that one is opaque. The city windows' lit layer covers only the windows' area, still trees only the trees' area, and the water and meadow strips reach only the bottom edge. A picture that changes often is one picture with its frames as cells, so a new frame moves the window into it instead of making and uploading a new picture (the synthwave palms: 33 frames each). A page whose background covers it fills no ground under it. A still view stands still: only the breathing colon, the second hands and the seconds line redraw (30 times a second), and only while their page is on screen (with a part of it during a swipe) and the screensaver is off; clocks, the nixie glow and the news crawls of the pages out of sight do not run. The synthwave scenes' cars and palms are planned once a second only while a scene is on screen: the scene time comes from Slint's animation clock, and every read of `animation-tick()` asks for a frame, so the app reads it only then. A binding that uses an animated value twice behind a condition (`a && f(x) && g(x)`) is split into two properties: the Slint compiler computes a twice-used operand before the condition, so the animated value is read in every frame anyway (an animated detail of one clock face kept every clock face at 60 frames a second). **Calm clocks**: when nobody has been near (proximity sensor) and nothing was asked of the panel (a touch, a Home Assistant request: settings, views, commands, notifications, the screen; not the live preview's screenshots) for 10 minutes, every clock (widget, clock page, screensaver) turns calm: gradually over 5 s the breathing colon becomes a plain step, bright one second and dim the next, and the seconds tick once a second on the whole second, so a clock draws one frame a second instead of 30; the nixie tubes glow steadily. On a whole second the breath is at its top or bottom, so calm and breathing meet without a jump. Anything that stirs the panel brings the breathing back, gradually over 2 s (the app looks every 0.5 s, taps and requests at once). Texts that a parent re-evaluates every second (a running timer's row) take their new value only when it really differs, so their layers stay; a running time in neon is drawn digit by digit, each digit its own layer. The nixie stopwatch draws only what lights, its hundredths in every frame: the case, the plinth, the glass and the sockets are one layer behind the tubes, the anode meshes, the highlights and the neon dots one layer in front; a tube takes new digit pictures only when its own digit changes (a binding that depends on the running time is marked dirty at every step even when its value stays), and it looks them up by their path, so every tube shows the one texture of that digit that the image cache keeps on the GPU (a picture held in a list property is the file's own copy, which the renderer makes into a new texture each time it is shown anew). A running stopwatch makes frames only where its time is drawn.
- **Pictures drawn by the app** (`src/draw.rs`): the label maker's tapes, the notepad's handwriting, the fridge notes, the wall calendar's pen notes and the mixing desk's labels. Skia draws them on the CPU in a thread of their own, random but stable for each item (seeded by its text, a wall calendar day by its date), and the page shows each as soon as it is ready; drawn ones are kept.
- **Texts**: the interface's word lists live in `src/text.pak` (read by `texts.rs`).
- **Assets**: pictures and fonts live in the UI bundle (`/opt/panelos-ui/share/ui`) and are read at run time by their path there; the image keeps a small fallback app for panels without the bundle.
- **Brand**: the PanelOS palette and type in `theme.slint`; Signal marks lit things only (dots, an active state, one highlighted number), never a text ground; button text is Ink or Paper.

## Pairing, theme, brightness, approach

- **Pairing by QR code** (no token typing): on first start the panel shows a QR code with Home Assistant's normal login page (OAuth authorization-code flow, the panel is the IndieAuth client: `client_id` `http://<panel-ip>:8080/`, `redirect_uri` `http://<panel-ip>:8080/auth/callback`; Home Assistant accepts local IP client IDs). After the login on the phone, Home Assistant redirects the phone to the panel, which exchanges the one-time code for a refresh token. The token is stored on the panel only (`/mnt/persist/nspanel/ha_auth.json`, root-only); it can be revoked in the Home Assistant user profile, after which the panel shows the QR code again.
- **Theme**: dark, light, or auto (the default, from the first start, setup and pairing screens included). What switches Auto is set in the integration (`theme_auto`): the light in the room (the default), the sun, or set times. By the light the sensor decides, dark below 10 lx, light above 30 lx, unchanged in between, and a change only after the light stayed on the other side for 10 s; the first decision is immediate, before the first frame. By the sun it is dark while Home Assistant's `sun.sun` is below the horizon; by set times dark from one time to the other (20:00 to 7:00 by default, across midnight too). Without what the trigger needs (no sun state yet, the clock not set) the light sensor decides, without a sensor reading the sun, and without either the theme stays as it is (dark at the start). A switch by someone crossfades the whole screen in 0.7 s; a change Auto makes by itself blends over 3 s.
- **Brightness**: one scale for automatic and manual brightness, even in perceived brightness (gamma 2.2), from 0.8 % (still readable at night, no flicker) to 100 %. Automatic: STK3x3x ambient light, 1 lx and darker 0.8 %, 10 lx 16 %, 50 lx 41 %, 150 lx 66 %, 500 lx and brighter 100 %; the sensor's scale is switched automatically because it saturates at about 6500 lx on its most sensitive scale. Manual: 1..100 % on the panel and in Home Assistant. Changes fade every 20 ms in perceived brightness (about 0.8 s down, 0.25 s up, 0.12 s when waking from the screensaver).
- **Approach wake**: the proximity sensor runs at its most sensitive gain, 11.84 ms integration time and the IR LED at full power (`hw.rs`). The app learns the resting level and its jitter (the mean change between consecutive resting readings) and reports an approach after two readings, half a second apart, above the resting level plus the signal of a hand at the wake distance (`PROXIMITY_WAKE_CM`, 40 cm, from the measured distance curve), or plus 5 times the jitter if that is more: noisy readings shorten the range instead of waking the panel. A slow approach does not raise the threshold, since it changes each reading only a little, and readings above half the threshold teach nothing (a hand held just below it would pull it up); whatever stays in front of the sensor for over a minute becomes the new resting level. The wake distance is set in the integration (`wake_distance`): near (30 cm), medium (40 cm, the default) or far (45 cm); presence uses the same threshold. **Calibration** (started from the integration): a black screen counts down 10 s to step back, then shows a still line while the panel takes 20 readings in 10 s (the pages under it are not drawn and nothing on the screen moves meanwhile: redraws can disturb the readings), then says how far the panel now wakes, to the nearest 5 cm. The resting level becomes the readings' median and the jitter the value the learning settles at for their consecutive changes (the largest tenth left out); with a reading above half the resulting threshold (something moved in front) nothing changes and the panel says so. A tap ends it at any time, nothing changed. Measurements and the test view: `docs/bringup.md`.
- **Child lock** (a switch of the panel's device in Home Assistant): no touch reaches the pages, the shade, the status bar or any layer. A tap shows the lock card (the card and hold ring of the panel editing's lock); holding the lock for 3 s unlocks the panel, and it locks again after a minute without a touch or when the screensaver starts. A tap outside the card or its X closes the card, and it closes by itself after 6 s. Voice, the approach wake and Home Assistant's requests work as usual; a tap on the screensaver wakes the screen; a ringing timer or alarm still stops with a tap, and while a critical alert is up the lock steps aside so it can be held. The touch test (from the integration) works over it. Switching the lock on closes the shade, the settings and any page menu.
- Icons: Material Design Icons 7.4.47 (the set Home Assistant uses), `mdi:` names from the entity or Home Assistant-like defaults per domain.
- **Screensaver**: one of the clock faces (style from the integration), or screen off; wakes on tap (on release, so the tap does not reach the page) or on approach; "Screen off" in the quick shade turns the screen dark until the next touch or approach. It starts after the idle time only inside its window, set in the integration: when the room is dark (the default: below 3 lx from the panel's light sensor or a Home Assistant one), between two times, from sunset to sunrise, or any time. Outside its window it gives way to the pages again (in the dark: once it is clearly brighter). It never starts while a timer runs or a status screen is up.

## Dashboard mode: web view

- A secondary function: the panel is made for the assistant first. The web view runs within the limits of four Cortex-A35 cores and a Mali-G31; a light, simple dashboard made for the panel works best.
- With the web bundle mounted (`/opt/panelos-web`, see `buildroot/configs/panelos_web_defconfig`), dashboard mode shows the Home Assistant dashboard chosen in the integration in WPE WebKit 2.50 (Release build, JavaScript JIT up to FTL, Skia, Mesa Panfrost on the Mali-G31). `src/web.rs` starts the `panelos-web` helper chrooted in the bundle. Login like the Home Assistant apps (external auth), the token only for the dashboard's own origin; another site never opens in the view (see [security.md](security.md#web-view)). Header and sidebar can be hidden.
- **Over the dashboard.** A ringing timer or alarm, an open status screen, a critical alert and a doorbell's visitor view take the web view off the screen (`web-covered` in `app.slint`): their pages show, as in Assistant mode, and the dashboard comes back when they end.
- **Direct frames.** At start the app creates three more display buffers (`Display::create_web_buffers`) and hands them to the helper as dma-bufs (`BUFS`). While the web view is all there is on the screen (no loading screen, pull to refresh, shade, settings, voice view, notification window, alert strip, ringing timer, screensaver, setup screen, touch or proximity test, proximity calibration, child lock card or start-up picture; checked on every turn of the event loop), the helper draws each WebKit frame into a free one on the GPU and the display flips to it (`FRAMEBUF`, `FREE`); the UI does not draw at all, except for up to a second after the web view takes the screen over and until its first frame (WebKit draws only when the page changes, so what just went off a still dashboard, such as a strip or its fading, would stay on the screen). With anything on top the frames go through shared memory as images under the UI's layers (`DIRECT 0` also hands over the frame on screen).
- **Loading screen.** WebKit's load start (`LOADING`) puts `loading.slint` (the PanelOS loading animation) over the web view. The helper checks the page every 300 ms through the shadow roots (Home Assistant's `hui-root`): ready (`READY`) when cards are drawn, the element count is the same three times in a row, icons have their drawing and images are in (icons waited for at most 20 s after the dashboard appeared, a view without cards 10 s), and no new frames came for 700 ms (or 4 s passed); at most 45 s. Then the screen fades in 450 ms. Measured on a dashboard of 15 cards: Home Assistant's frontend takes ~15 s to start, the cards come at 20–27 s. The integration option `loading_screen` turns it off (Home Assistant's own start-up shows instead).
- **Size.** The integration option `zoom` reaches the helper as `ZOOM` (WebKit's zoom level).
- **Pull to refresh.** A touch that starts in the top 24 px is the settings swipe and never reaches the page. Any other touch goes to the page, and at touch down the app asks the helper (`ATTOP x y`) whether the content under the finger is scrolled to its top (the nearest scrolling element through shadow roots and slots, or the page). If it is, a pull of 16 px straight down starts pull to refresh: a dark disc comes down with the finger and the mark writes itself in it; after 110 px more, letting go reloads the page (`RELOAD`, behind the loading screen). Scrolling up or moving sideways first is not a pull.
- **Fonts.** DejaVu Sans plus Noto Sans (package `noto-sans`, from Google Fonts at a fixed commit) as a wide fallback: cards use characters DejaVu and Roboto lack, e.g. Mushroom's separator U+2E31.
- **Skipped resources.** Home Assistant loads every card resource on every dashboard. The integration works out the ones the dashboard shown does not need (`resources.py`; the panel reports which dashboard it shows) and sends them with the settings; they reach the helper as `SKIP`; it blocks those URLs with a WebKit content filter, and the page load waits until the filter is ready.
- Measured on the panel (scroll test page with touches from the development input; the dashboard scrolled by a script, no touches):

  | | before | direct frames |
  |---|---|---|
  | frame to the app (read back / GPU copy) | ~30 ms | ~2.5–3.5 ms |
  | frame made → on screen | ~52 ms | ~13–21 ms |
  | frames per second while swiping (test page) | 14–25 | 38–56 |
  | dashboard, scripted scroll | 16–17 /s | 16–20 /s (limited by the page's own work) |

  First load of a dashboard with 35 card resources (8.9 MB of JavaScript): ~21 s with everything, ~15–17 s with the 33 it does not need skipped. Loading is limited by the CPU (scripts, style, layout); the GPU is busy at most 13 % of the time. Skia painting on the CPU (3 threads) instead of the GPU made no clear difference.
- Still on the page side: heavy card code and effects (blur behind a bar, many scripts) cost on four Cortex-A35 cores; the page stays loaded between uses (switching modes does not reload it).

## ESPHome native API

- The app is an ESPHome API server on TCP 6053 (`esphome.rs`; transport in `api_link.rs`): only Home Assistant's address may connect, and Home Assistant gives the panel a key after adding it and connects encrypted from then on (Noise, as ESPHome devices; details in [security.md](security.md)). Message types are generated from `app/proto/api.proto` (aioesphomeapi, MIT). Home Assistant's built-in ESPHome integration adds the panel by IP, or by discovery.
- mDNS (`app/src/mdns.rs`, no system daemon): the panel announces `<panel ID>._esphomelib._tcp` (`nspanel-pro` for a panel without a name, see [Configuration](#configuration); port 6053; TXT `friendly_name`, `version`, `mac`, `network`, `project_name`, `project_version`, the same values as in the API's device info), answers queries for it and for `<panel ID>.local`, and announces again after an address change. Home Assistant matches the `mac` record with a configured device and only updates its address. mDNS stays inside one network segment unless the router repeats it.
- Entities on the ESPHome device (the voice satellite): Volume (number), Mute microphone (switch), Media player (see below), Wake level, Wake SNR, Wake score (sensors; sent with every wake word before the voice run starts, so Home Assistant can let the satellite that heard best answer: level = RMS of the last second in dBFS, SNR = loudest 100 ms of the last second over the second before it in dB, score = the model's averaged probability), Panel user (text sensor, diagnostic: the id of the Home Assistant user the panel is logged in as, asked with `auth/current_user` on every connection of `ha.rs`; the PanelOS integration accepts commands as this panel only from that user and administrators, see [security.md](security.md#home-assistant-integration)), plus the voice satellite entities Home Assistant creates itself. The device is named "NSPanel Pro Voice" (with the panel's name for a panel that has one, e.g. "NSPanel Pro Voice (kitchen)"), maker PanelOS, model Voice satellite; its ESPHome device name is the panel's ID.
- `src/esphome.rs`: framing, entity list, state diffing (changes pushed when the UI thread notifies, or when the Home Assistant client sets the panel's user), commands mapped to the same setting keys as the on-panel settings screen.

## The panel's device (PanelOS integration)

- The display, the settings and the sensors are entities of the PanelOS integration's device ("NSPanel Pro", or e.g. "NSPanel Pro (kitchen)" for a panel with a name, maker PanelOS, model NSPanel Pro 86 Gen1), which the ESPHome device is connected via: Screen (switch: off = screensaver now), Dashboard (select, options = the dashboards the panel's user may open), Theme, Screensaver after, Screensaver style, Display mode, Time format (selects), Brightness (number), Automatic brightness, Wake on approach, Child lock, Rain on the glass, Lightning (switches), Illuminance, SoC temperature, Wi-Fi signal, IP address, App version (sensors), Presence (binary sensor from the proximity sensor), Restart app, Reboot (buttons), Notifications (notify, see *Shown by Home Assistant*).
- `src/panel_device.rs` turns the device state into the report and the commands into settings; `src/ha.rs` sends `panelos/report` over the panel's Home Assistant connection once the integration has accepted its subscription (device info once, then only changed states and select options, checked every 200 ms) and passes the `command` events on. The protocol is in [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md#how-it-talks-to-the-panel).
- Recorded sensors are sent only after a set change (`report_device` in `main.rs`, steps in `settings.rs` `Reporting`, set in the integration's options): illuminance by a share of the last value sent (default 15 %, at least 5 lx), SoC temperature (1 °C) and Wi-Fi signal (3 dB) on five-minute averages of once-a-minute readings. Details in [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md#sensor-reporting).
- rtw88 does not fill `/proc/net/wireless`; the Wi-Fi signal comes from `iw dev wlan0 link`.

## Voice satellite

- The panel is an Assist satellite through the same ESPHome API connection (`VOICE_FEATURES` = voice assistant, speaker, API audio, timers, announcements; Home Assistant only offers the wake word selects when the announce flag is set). Home Assistant shows it as an assist satellite with Assistant / Assistant 2 and Wake word / Wake word 2 selects, like ESPHome voice satellites. The panel reports its wake words to the integration (`wake_words` in `panelos/report`), which keeps those selects and Home Assistant's configuration of the satellite in line with them (`custom_components/panelos/wake_words.py`; Home Assistant itself takes a satellite's wake word into the selects only when it has exactly one, and a select set alone sends the panel only the words the selects show).
- **Wake words** run on the panel: microWakeWord models downloaded by `tools/build_image.sh` into `/usr/share/nspanel/wakewords`: `okay_nabu` (OHF-Voice release 20241226.3, cutoff 0.85), `hey_jarvis`, `hey_mycroft`, `alexa`, and the experimental `hey_home_assistant`, `okay_computer`, `choo_choo_homie`, `hey_peppa_pig`. The internal `stop` model is not offered in Home Assistant: it listens only while the panel speaks or a timer rings (and 2 s after), and stops it. Home Assistant's Wake word selects list what the panel reports; any microWakeWord model (`<id>.tflite` + `<id>.json`) in that folder appears there. Only enabled models run: the idle app takes 6.9 % of one core with two wake words. TensorFlow Lite (Buildroot package) and the TFLite micro frontend are compiled into the app (`app/native/`).
- **Wake word reset**: the streaming models keep their recent input inside the model (resource variables), so after a conversation the wake word that started it would fire again as soon as listening resumes. `mww_reset` feeds 3 s of silence features through every model.
- **Echo cancellation**: WebRTC AudioProcessing 1.3 (AEC3 + high-pass filter, Buildroot package `webrtc-audio-processing`, `app/native/aec.cc`), 16 kHz mono, 10 ms frames. The reference is the player's own output (written to ALSA in 10 ms pieces, 48 kHz → 16 kHz, ALSA buffer 100 ms). Measured: microphone level 15–28 dB lower during a spoken answer; the panel's own voice saying a wake word does not wake it. It runs only while the speaker plays and 2 s after (continuously it would cost about a quarter of a core). With it the wake word also works while the panel speaks: it cuts the answer off and starts a new conversation. Noise suppression is off (the wake word frontend has its own; Home Assistant's STT gets the echo-free voice).
- **Audio**: in from the PDM microphones (`hw:CARD=pdmmics`), 4 channels at 16 kHz, the mono signal is the mean of channels 2 and 3 with a fixed gain; out to `hw:CARD=rk809` at 48 kHz (chimes, TTS, ring and alarm sounds; 16 kHz, WAV and MP3 sources are resampled; MP3 decoded with `minimp3`).
- **Do not disturb** without voice answers (`voice::DND_QUIET`, set by the UI from `dnd_allows(.., "voice")`): a conversation that starts then has no listening chime and its answer is not played (streamed audio is dropped, a TTS URL is not fetched); the voice view shows it as usual and, after the run, keeps a silent answer on screen a little longer for reading (`silent_answer_secs`: 5 s, 15 characters a second, at most 30 s; a tap closes it). The choice is made once, at the wake word: the conversation's follow-ups and an answer that arrives after do not disturb ends stay silent, and a conversation that started before do not disturb began keeps speaking; the next wake word decides again. The log says `voice: do not disturb: no chime, the answer only on the screen`.
- **Streamed answers**: Home Assistant sends the spoken answer over the API about as fast as it is spoken, with waits of up to about 0.7 s between chunks; the panel starts it once 0.8 s of audio is in (or after 1.5 s), and logs the chunk statistics, the longest wait (as the API receives the audio and as the voice thread does) and speaker underruns. The conversation stays on screen until the speech ends.
- **Flow**: wake word → `VoiceAssistantRequest` → audio over the API until Home Assistant's VAD ends the run → events (STT text, intent, TTS start/end) drive the conversation display. `continue_conversation` opens the microphone again for a follow-up. If nobody starts to speak within 5 s of a follow-up, the panel aborts the run (`VoiceAssistantRequest start=false`: Home Assistant cancels the pipeline, no answer); otherwise Home Assistant's segmenter would wait 15 s. Speech start is Home Assistant's STT-VAD-start event or the panel's own level detector (10 ms frames 12 dB over the room level for ~150 ms; the room level is followed while the panel is idle and silent), so a reply that begins just before the 5 s mark is not cut off. Home Assistant's API TTS stream can be empty (a WAV of unknown length), so the panel fetches the TTS URL from the TTS-end event itself (`/api/tts_proxy/<token>.wav`).
- **Timers** (Assist timer events): nearest timer in the top bar, full-screen alarm for at most a minute with the timers page's sound (set in the integration, else its look's own: Marimba, Mechanical bell, Glass chimes, Synth, Digital), looping until it is stopped (a tap anywhere stops it; its second button gives a timer one more minute and puts an alarm to sleep for 9 minutes, after which it rings again); alarms ring the same way with their own sound (Birds by default), a critical alert with its own (SOS by default) until it is held or clears. The sounds (`app/src/sounds.rs`): the panel's own in the UI bundle (`ui/sounds/*.wav`), uploaded ones fetched from Home Assistant into `/mnt/data/panelos-sounds` and kept as long as a setting uses them. An alarm with music asks Music Assistant (`music_assistant.play_media`) to play on the panel's own media player and rises from quiet to full over 30 s; without audio after 12 s its default sound rings. By voice: a wake word or a "Stop" heard by the panel stops a ring at once (a "Stop" in its first 1.5 s is checked by the speech recognition); what someone says over the ring (from 8 dB over what is left of its echo) goes to the speech recognition, and only a stop word or a short stop phrase stops it (the panel knows English and Hungarian ones), nothing goes to the assistant. When a timer starts ringing during a voice run, the run's sentence stops it only if the whole sentence is a stop phrase (a stop word or a two-word stop phrase, with at most a few small words around it); any other sentence, however short ("no", "thanks", or a command such as "turn off the light"), goes on to the assistant. The ring shows on the timers page (a temporary one when the page list has none; leaving a page closes its menus); when it stops or its minute is up, the panel goes back to the page it was on, unless someone paged meanwhile.
- **Voice view**: the full-screen conversation in the integration's look (plain, terminal, comic, oscilloscope). Each state reads apart in every look. The microphone level and the traces are measured only while the view shows (`voice::METER_ON`); closing the view (its time is up, or a tap) stops them.
- **Media player** (`app/src/media.rs`): an ESPHome media player entity (formats: WAV, 48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo, for music and announcements; pause, volume, mute, stop, play, announce). Home Assistant's media proxy converts any source to that WAV and the panel streams it (half a second buffered first) to the speaker; the volume is a share of the panel's volume. While a timer or an alarm rings the panel listens itself, the ring playing on unchanged: a wake word or a "Stop" from the model stops it at once, except that a "Stop" in the ring's first 1.5 s goes to the speech recognition with the 1.5 s before it (at a ring's start the echo canceller learns the new sound for about a second, and its leftover can sound like "Stop"; over a ring the speech recognition mostly hears nothing in a real "Stop"); speech over the ring (the processed microphone signal 8 dB over what is left of the ring, its level over the last 2 s, for 60 ms) is cut out with half a second before it until 0.6 s of quiet (at most 2.5 s) and sent to the speech recognition of the panel's own assistant (`POST /api/stt/<engine>`, the engine and language from the integration), outside any conversation, made louder first (at most 30 times, never clipped) and, when the assistant's language is not English, recognized in English at the same time (a Hungarian recognition mostly hears nothing in an English "Stop"): only a short phrase with a stop word (such as "stop", "enough", "quiet", "turn off") stops the ring, and nothing goes to the assistant. The log notes the ring's leftover level, each piece of speech with its text, and why a ringing stopped (tap, voice, more time, a minute).
- **Announcements** (`assist_satellite.announce`) play and report back.
- **Sendspin player** (`app/src/sendspin.rs`): the panel is a player of Music Assistant through Sendspin, Music Assistant's own playback protocol (https://github.com/Sendspin/spec), so Music Assistant has it without any setup. The panel announces `_sendspin._tcp` over mDNS (port 8928, path `/sendspin`, name "NSPanel Pro", with the panel's name for a panel that has one) and Music Assistant connects to it. Every connection is end-to-end encrypted with Noise `KKpsk2_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256` (the panel is the responder); unpaired access is off. Pairing needs nobody: the panel reports its Sendspin client id among its ESPHome device capabilities (API 1.15, `DeviceCapabilitiesResponse`), Home Assistant asks its pairing token over the encrypted API (`SendspinPairingTokenRequest`, refused without the API key) and hands it to Music Assistant, which pairs with it (Pairing PSK flow) and connects with the long-term key from then on. The key pair, the pairing PSK and up to 8 pairing records are in `/mnt/persist/nspanel/sendspin.json` (0600). Audio: PCM 48 kHz 16-bit stereo chunks stamped with the server's clock; the spec's time filter (8 exchanges every 5 s, the best one fed in) maps them to the panel's clock, and the speaker thread mixes the music in when it is due (it measures the ALSA delay for each 10 ms piece; small errors are corrected by one frame per 10 ms, large ones by a jump), under every other sound and into the echo canceller's reference. While the assistant listens or thinks the music is silent; it plays on under the answer. Music Assistant's volume of the panel is the panel's own volume (both ways), mute is Music Assistant's own. A session that has not paired (the pairing PSK, or the sentinel PSK any host on the network gets) can only pair: it never holds the panel, never starts, clears or ends the music, its audio chunks are dropped and it never sets the music's clock. Only the paired server that holds the panel as a player feeds the time filter, and a new one starts it afresh (another server's clock base); a session that hears nothing for 30 s (the clock exchanges answer every 5 s) ends. Music Assistant shows the panel as one player of its device (with the Sendspin player as its output); the music page lists it first, as "This panel". When the app stops (the service stopping, or its restart button) it says goodbye with "restart" first, so Music Assistant connects again as soon as the app is back. Log: `sendspin: connected / activate / stream / playing, … ms buffered, music … dBFS`. Measured: paired 1 s after the first connection; 5 s buffered while playing.
- **Volume**: the quick shade's volume slider and the `Volume` number entity in Home Assistant (0–100 %, step 5, default 80 %). It sets the RK809 "Master Playback Volume": 100 % = 0 dB, −0.4 dB per percent, 0 % = off. A short chime plays at the new level.
- **Mute microphone**: switch in Home Assistant and on the panel (red icon in the top bar).
- Logging and testing: every pipeline event is logged (`Assist event <type> key=value…`, `voice: run end, follow-up yes/no`). `/tmp/nspanel-voice-test.wav` (16 kHz mono) is sent as the speech after the next wake word, `/tmp/nspanel-voice-followup.wav` as the speech of the next follow-up, so whole conversations can be replayed. `NSPANEL_AEC_DUMP=<file>` writes microphone and processed samples interleaved.

## Shown by Home Assistant

- **Integration actions**: `panelos.show_view` and `panelos.notify` (below). A view: `home` is the start page (the first in the pager); `weather`, `media` and `camera` go to the page of that kind, a temporary page when the pager has none; `camera` with `entity_id` goes to the cameras page that has that camera (the one on screen first) and shows it big, and a camera no cameras page has comes alone on the temporary cameras page, in a row of `cam-inst` after the cameras pages' rows, named as the integration sends it (`cam::show_camera`, `cam::guest_row`); `dashboard` is the web view. A page view comes over dashboard mode's web view, the web view over assistant mode's pages. It wakes the screen and keeps the screensaver away while it is up. With seconds the panel goes back to the page it was on when they run out, unless someone paged meanwhile (a timer ringing then keeps the panel until the ring ends), and the display mode comes back. With 0 the view holds until the next finger goes down on the screen (`platform::touches`); from then on the panel is as usual, and a display mode the view changed comes back once nobody has used the panel for the screensaver's time (two minutes without a screensaver; `visit::back_after`). `app/src/visit.rs`, `handle_show` in `main.rs`.
- **PanelOS notifications**: the integration's `notify` entity per panel (`notify.send_message`) and the `panelos.notify` action (panels, title, message, icon, level, a picture from a camera or an address, up to three buttons, until answered) put a notification into the panels' lists and their windows, next to Home Assistant's persistent notifications. The integration keeps each one until it is answered (a panel that comes back gets the unread ones again); OK or a button marks it read on that panel, a button also fires `panelos_notification_action`, Dismiss removes it everywhere.
- **Requests from the integration's rules** (same path): `status` / `status_clear` (id, category, own headline / line / pictogram / colour, button, countdown), `critical` / `critical_clear` (id, title, message, icon, source, whether holding silences it), `touch_test` (starts the touch calibration).
- **Doorbell**: with a `ring` binary sensor on the cameras page, the panel wakes on that page when it rings, the picture big, a chime (if do not disturb lets it through) and, with a `lock` on the page, "hold to open"; it goes after a minute or with ×.
- **Replies at the door**: with a speaker at the door on the cameras page (`speaker`, any media player; at first the one on a camera's own device), the visitor window shows a microphone. A tap grows a card of up to four replies out of it (`replies`, each one line; four defaults until set); a tap on one has Home Assistant say it on that speaker (`tts.speak` with the panel assistant's text-to-speech engine, in the page's `language` or the assistant's language and voice), the row lights and the card goes back into the microphone. The visitor window then stays another minute. No microphone without a speaker, replies or a text-to-speech engine.
- **Camera page settings**: from the quick shade's page row ("Camera settings · Cameras"), the brand card under the status bar: Picture (whole picture / fill the screen, kept by the integration) and Show (one camera / all cameras, as the chip); a change shows under the card at once.
- **Configuration** comes from the PanelOS integration in `custom_components/panelos` (see [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md)): the page list (kind, look, entities, options per page), the weather and media player, the voice view's look, the dashboard for dashboard mode. The panel subscribes with `panelos/subscribe` and keeps the last configuration in `panel-config.json` beside `settings.json`, so a restart needs no reconnect. While the integration is missing the panel asks again every 10 s, so installing it needs no panel restart.
- The voice assistant works in both display modes; its view is full screen in both (unless switched off in the integration).

## Connection, start and outages

- **First screen with current content**: the Home Assistant client starts before the app takes the display over from the boot animation. The app waits until the clock is set and the first full state list is in (plus 300 ms for the assistant settings and forecasts); at most 25 s, at once when pairing is needed. Meanwhile the boot animation stays on screen.
- **Clock**: the panel has no battery-backed clock and starts in 1970. Until NTP has set it, the app takes the time from the `Date` header of Home Assistant's WebSocket handshake (`app/src/clock.rs`), which also works without internet access. Until either has set it, the UI shows `--:--` and no date.
- **Time zone** (`app/src/timezone.rs`): the panel follows Home Assistant's time zone. The panel has no zone database: the PanelOS integration sends the zone with the panel's settings as its POSIX TZ rule (`time_zone`: `name` and `posix`, e.g. `CET-1CEST,M3.5.0,M10.5.0/3`), and the app puts the rule in `TZ` and calls `tzset` (glibc reads `TZ` once; `localtime_r` does not look again). Every local time in the app goes through the C library, so the clocks, the screensaver, alarms and their snooze, timers, the calendar, to-do dates, notification times and the connection log all follow it. A change of Home Assistant's zone reaches a running panel with its settings: the times on screen are written again at once, the calendar is read again (its six weeks and all-day events are local dates), and the web view's helper is started again (it takes the zone when it starts; the page loads again). The last zone is kept with the integration's settings (`panel-config.json`), so a panel that starts without Home Assistant keeps its zone; before its first settings ever (or with an integration that sends no zone) the panel uses Central European time. A zone change is written to the connection log (`time zone: …`). Development: `{"config": {"time_zone": {"name": "…", "posix": "…"}}}` on the dev FIFO sets another zone until the app restarts.
- **NTP** (`S45ntp`): busybox `ntpd` starts as soon as Wi-Fi has an address, however late that is, and is started again 30 s after it exits.
- **Retries, for good** (a power cut can keep Home Assistant or the network away for hours): without network every 0.5 s during the first minute after the start, then every 5 s; while Home Assistant does not answer 2, 4, 8, then every 10 s. A keep-alive goes both ways every second, and a vanished server is noticed after 8 s without anything from it. A failure is logged once per cause, the recovery with the length of the outage.
- **Connection log** (`app/src/connlog.rs`): Home Assistant sessions ending (with the cause) and coming back, every roam check, move, link probe and link watch reconnect, one line each with the local time, in `/mnt/persist/nspanel/connection.log` (moved to `connection.log.1` at 128 KB, so the last ~256 KB stay). The system log lives in RAM, so a drop in the night can be looked at only here.
- **A short drop in Home Assistant**: the integration keeps the panel's entities (all but the restart and reboot buttons) with their last state for 30 s after the panel went away (a Wi-Fi hiccup, an app restart) and turns them unavailable only after that.
- **Status line**: "Waiting for the network…", "Home Assistant not reachable, retrying…", or Home Assistant's own error (login, dashboard).
- **Wi-Fi roam check** (`app/src/roam.rs`): from 5 minutes after the start, about every 30 minutes, and only while the panel is idle (no voice run, no awaited or playing answer, no announcement, no ringing timer, no Sendspin music; otherwise again a minute later). At -49 dBm or better there is no scan. Otherwise the panel scans for its own network by name (`wpa_cli scan ssid <hex>`, plain `wpa_cli scan` if that is refused; results after 8 s; an access point that hides the network name answers only such a scan) and moves with `wpa_cli roam` to the strongest access point of the same network if it is at least 10 dB stronger than the current one as the same scan saw it. `wpa_cli status` and `wpa_cli scan_results` print the network name escaped (wpa_supplicant's `printf_encode`: `\xNN` for bytes outside printable ASCII, `\\`, `\"`, `\e`, `\n`, `\r`, `\t`); the check decodes it to the name's own bytes, so the scan asks for the real name (accents, emoji, quotes and spaces included) and the scan results are matched on the same bytes. The thresholds are those of ESPHome's `wifi.roam`. The move is one-shot: the network is never tied to one access point, so a panel moved to another room keeps working. Each check writes one line (`roam check: …`) with the access points of the network it saw, a move also the result 15 s later. Tested on the panel: moved by hand (`wpa_cli roam`) to an access point about 20 dB weaker, the first check moved it back; the re-association took about 1 s, and the Home Assistant connections stayed up through both moves. A strong signal is not always a good link: an access point can lose most of its frames to the panel at full signal while a weaker one carries the same data without loss. So every 5 minutes while idle the panel also pings its router 20 times (0.2 s apart); two poor probes in a row (15 % or more lost, or 150 ms or more on average; no answer at all does not count, that is the link watch's case) make it avoid that access point for two hours and move to the best other one of the network (-75 dBm or better). Every move is probed the same way, and a poor new access point means a move back. The 30-minute check never picks an avoided access point; after two hours every access point counts again (`/tmp/panelos-poor-aps`, kept over app restarts until the next boot). Music counts as busy because a move drops the stream for a moment; a panel that fell to a farther access point during music would stay there as long as the music lasts. So a stall (`sendspin: stall, …`: the music had played for 5 s, then the buffer stayed dry for a second; the gap after a start or a seek does not count) starts the check within 5 s during the music when nothing else is going on (`roam check: the music stalls, …`), also in the first 5 minutes after the start, at most every 10 minutes. The move still needs an access point at least 10 dB stronger, and at -49 dBm or better there is no scan. Development: `{"music_stall": true}` on the dev FIFO counts as a stall. Roaming can be switched off in the integration (`wifi_roaming`, on by default): then there are no roam checks, no stall checks and no link probes; the link watch (a link that carries nothing is made again) and the Wi-Fi power save staying off do not depend on it. The change is written to the connection log.
- Tested on the panel: Home Assistant gone for 75 s → connected again 9 s after it returned; start without Home Assistant and internet → UI after 25 s with `--:--`, NTP set the clock 8 s after the internet came back, Home Assistant connected 6–9 s after it returned.

- **Changing the Wi-Fi network** (`app/src/wifi.rs`, settings → Wi-Fi or the setup screen's Wi-Fi card): the new network is added to the running wpa_supplicant beside the current one (the passphrase turned into its key by `wpa_passphrase` on its standard input, WPA3-only networks with SAE) and selected. It counts as connected when the panel is associated (25 s at most), the DHCP client has given it an address after letting the old lease go (20 s) and Home Assistant answers at its address (three tries). Only then is it written to the saved config, `/mnt/persist/nspanel/wpa_supplicant.conf` (and its running copy), as the network of priority 1, with the one it replaces kept as a fallback of priority 0; the file is written whole to a temporary file, synced and renamed over the old one. Otherwise the panel selects the old network again, forgets the new one and says why (a wrong password, the network did not answer, no address, Home Assistant not answering there); the saved config is not touched. A saved network picked again is selected the same way. The roam check and the link watch wait while a switch runs. Each switch writes its steps to the connection log (`wifi: …`).

## First start

Without `app.json` the app shows the setup screen: Home Assistant instances found over mDNS (`_home-assistant._tcp`; one shows with its name and address, several as a count that opens a list to pick from) and a keypad for the address (digits, `.`, `:`). Without a port it tries 8123, then 80. It checks that Home Assistant answers (`/auth/providers` needs no login and only exists there), writes `app.json` and restarts the app, which then shows the pairing QR code.

## Configuration

`/mnt/persist/nspanel/app.json` holds the connection settings; the login token is kept separately in `ha_auth.json`.

```json
{"ha_url": "http://192.168.1.10:8123"}
```

- `ha_url`: the address of Home Assistant, with the port if it uses one (Home Assistant OS installed since 2026.8 listens on port 80; older installations and containers on 8123). Use the IP address: the panel does not resolve `.local` (mDNS) names.
- `dashboard`: url_path of the dashboard to show; empty or missing = the default dashboard. A dashboard chosen with the panel's Dashboard select in Home Assistant comes first (kept in `settings.json`).
- `http_port` (default 8080): port of the login callback server.
- `api_allow`: further addresses allowed to use the ESPHome API besides Home Assistant's own (see [security.md](security.md)).

`/mnt/persist/nspanel/panel_name` holds the panel's name (one line, written by the install guide's step 9), its ID everywhere (`app/src/panel_id.rs`): `panel_id` in every message to the PanelOS integration, the ESPHome device name and the mDNS names (`<name>._esphomelib._tcp`, `<name>._sendspin._tcp`, `<name>.local`), and the names of its devices ("NSPanel Pro (kitchen)", "NSPanel Pro Voice (kitchen)", the Sendspin player "NSPanel Pro (kitchen)"). A name has letters a-z, digits and hyphens, at most 31 characters, no hyphen at either end; upper-case letters count as lower case. Without the file, with an empty one or with a name outside these rules (the log says why) the panel is `nspanel-pro` with the names "NSPanel Pro" and "NSPanel Pro Voice". It is read once at the start; settings → About shows it as the Panel ID.

## Build, run, develop

```bash
tools/build_image.sh       # builds host Rust + fontconfig, then the app (tools/build_app.sh), then the image with the app
tools/build_app.sh         # app only -> /root/nspanel/app-build/target/aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu/release/nspanel-app
GPU=1 tools/build_app.sh   # the GPU app (feature gpu) -> /root/nspanel/app-build-gpu/…; both builds copy app/ui to /opt/panelos-ui/share/ui
tools/build_web.sh         # the web bundle -> /root/nspanel/br-web/images/rootfs.squashfs (hours the first time)
tools/build_ui_bundle.sh   # the GPU app, then the UI bundle -> /root/nspanel/out/panelos-ui.squashfs (BUILD=0: packs the last GPU build)
tools/install_bundles.sh <panel IP> [ui] [web]   # puts the bundles on a panel over SSH (both without ui / web; DRY_RUN=1: only the plan)
```

- The image starts the app from `/etc/init.d/S90nspanel-app` (restarted when it exits, output to syslog).
- The GPU interface runs from two bundles, SquashFS images (zstd) on the panel's storage:
  - the web bundle (`tools/build_web.sh`, `buildroot/configs/panelos_web_defconfig`): its `rootfs.squashfs` as `/mnt/persist/panelos-web.squashfs`, mounted at `/opt/panelos-web` (`S50panelos-web`); it brings WPE WebKit and the GPU libraries (Mesa Panfrost, GBM);
  - the UI bundle (`tools/build_ui_bundle.sh`): `bin/nspanel-app`, the GPU app, and `share/ui/`, the app's `ui/` folder without the `.slint` files (they are compiled into the app). The app opens its pictures, fonts, sounds and layout data by their absolute path under `/opt/panelos-ui/share/ui` (`PANELOS_UI_DIR`, set by `tools/build_app.sh`). On the panel it is `/mnt/data/panelos-ui.squashfs` or `/mnt/persist/panelos-ui.squashfs`, mounted at `/opt/panelos-ui` (`S51panelos-ui`).
- The GPU app links the web bundle's libgbm and loads Mesa from it at run time: build the UI bundle after the web bundle, and again whenever the web bundle is built anew. `mksquashfs` comes from the web bundle's Buildroot host tools (`/root/nspanel/br-web/host/bin`), or from the distribution's `squashfs-tools`.
- `tools/install_bundles.sh` puts the UI bundle on `/mnt/data` when that partition is there (install guide, step 14), else on `/mnt/persist`, and the web bundle on `/mnt/persist`. Each file is uploaded under a temporary name next to its place and checked (size, md5) while the app runs. Then a script on the panel, detached from the SSH session, checks the uploads again, stops the app, unmounts the bundles, renames the new files into place, mounts them and starts the app, with the development switches (`NSPANEL_*`, `PANELOS_*`, `WEBKIT_*`) it ran with. Without room for a second copy the upload goes to `/mnt/data` and is copied into place, or, without room there either, the old bundle is removed first (it asks). A bundle that is already on the panel is skipped; an upload that is complete on the panel is not repeated.
- What runs, by what is on the panel:

  | On the panel | App |
  |---|---|
  | both bundles | the UI bundle's app, drawing on the GPU |
  | the UI bundle only | the image's app (the GPU app needs the web bundle's Mesa), with the bundle's pictures and fonts |
  | no UI bundle | the image's app with the system font and without the pages' pictures |

  The image's app is built with the software renderer and the same `PANELOS_UI_DIR`, so it takes its pictures and fonts from the UI bundle too; the image itself carries none of them, only DejaVu Sans. When the bundle's app ends within 30 s three times in a row, the image's app runs until the next reboot (`S90nspanel-app`, `/tmp/nspanel-gpu-off`).
- Development: a folder `/mnt/data/panelos-ui-dev` with the UI bundle's layout (`bin/nspanel-app`, `share/ui/`) is mounted instead of either bundle file when it exists. `tools/install_bundles.sh ui` still puts the file in place and says that the folder comes first; the file is used once the folder is moved away.
- Development aids:
  - `NSPANEL_CONFIG`, `NSPANEL_SETTINGS` and `NSPANEL_AUTH` name other files for `app.json`, `settings.json` (the files kept beside it follow it) and `ha_auth.json`: a second instance with a test Home Assistant leaves the panel's own files alone.
  - `NSPANEL_DEV_INPUT=/tmp/nspanel-input` creates a FIFO: lines `tap x y`, `down x y`, `move x y`, `up x y` act as touches, `swipe x1 y1 x2 y2 ms` is a swipe with a move every 16 ms, `pinch x y d1 d2 ms` two fingers one above the other round (x, y), d1 px apart, spreading to d2 px apart in `ms` (the second finger lands 40 ms after the first, a move every 16 ms, then both lift), JSON lines `{"web_load": url}`, `{"web_scroll_find": 1}` then `{"web_scroll": [px, ms]}` (a scripted scroll, no touches) and `{"web_skip": [path, ...]}` drive the web view for measurements, JSON lines are app commands (`{"show": {...}}` with the integration's fields, `{"assist": [state, heard, answer]}`, `{"page": n}`, `{"act": [what, value]}`, `{"config": {...}}` integration settings kept over Home Assistant's, `{"notes": {"kind": "added", "notes": {id: {...}}}}` a notification change as Home Assistant sends it, `{"connecting": true}`, `{"offline": n}`, `{"dark": true}`, `{"frame_stats": true}`, `{"glass_pass": false}` the rain on the glass's GPU pass off (`true` on again; `PANELOS_GLASS=0` starts without it), `{"saver": true}` the screensaver at once, `{"pages": ["kind:look", ...]}`, `{"wx": {...}}` weather values held over the real ones (`mood`, `rain`, `wind`, `gust`, `theme`, `sun_el`; with `rising` the synthwave views follow `sun_el` as a morning or an evening sun; `{"wx": {}}` lets them go), and test commands for the synthwave scene (`synth.rs`)).
  - `NSPANEL_FRAME_STATS=1` logs frames per second, frame intervals and render times every 5 s, and each frame over 40 ms with its parts (recording, flush, flip wait, swap). While drops are drawn on the glass, a line `glass` every 5 s: its frames a second, the rain level, the drops, beads and streak points, and the CPU time of its simulation and of its GPU pass (p50 / p99); the pass's own GPU time is part of the frame's render time.
  - `kill -USR1 <pid>` writes the screen to `/tmp/nspanel-screen.ppm`.

## Dependencies and gotchas

- Slint 1.18: the GPU app uses the Skia renderer (`i-slint-renderer-skia`, vendored with two changes, see `app/vendor/README.md`) on OpenGL ES through GBM and EGL (`gbm`, `glutin`); fonts and pictures come from the UI bundle. The image's fallback build uses `renderer-software` and `software-renderer-systemfonts` (runtime TrueType rendering, so any text from Home Assistant renders, accents included).
- `software-renderer-systemfonts` links **fontconfig**, and Slint's font backend (fontique 0.11) **panics if fontconfig finds no font at all**. The image therefore carries fontconfig and one system font (DejaVu Sans).
- Embedding pre-rendered glyphs (`EmbedForSoftwareRenderer`) was not chosen: it only embeds ASCII plus the characters of the UI's string literals, so dynamic text would miss glyphs.
- Other crates (`app/Cargo.toml`): drm, tungstenite (plain `ws://`), ureq 2 (plain HTTP), qrcode, jpeg-decoder, png, jpeg-encoder, serde_json, prost (the ESPHome API's messages), snow (Noise), sha2 and base64 (Sendspin), alsa, minimp3; in the GPU build also skia-safe (the app's own pictures) and glow (direct OpenGL calls, e.g. the rain on the glass). No TLS yet: an `https://` Home Assistant URL needs a TLS feature.
- The touchscreen shows as "Hynitron CST226SE Touchscreen" (kernel patch 0002 sets a per-chip input name; the mainline driver hard-codes "cst3xx").
