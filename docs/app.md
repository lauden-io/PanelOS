# Panel app (`app/`)

Native Rust app with the Slint UI toolkit. It draws its own interface on the panel's GPU and reads the touchscreen itself, without a compositor or window system; a web view comes in only in dashboard mode.

## Interface

- **Rendering**: Slint 1.18 with its Skia renderer on OpenGL ES (Mesa Panfrost on the Mali-G31, libraries from the web bundle), drawn into a GBM surface whose buffers go to the screen with page flips (`src/gpu.rs`; the GBM / EGL glue follows Slint's KMS backend). The app runs its own event loop (`src/platform.rs`): page flips, touch, wake-ups from other threads, timers. It renders the next frame while the last one waits for its flip, and only when something changed or an animation runs.
- **Pages**: the pages sit side by side in a pager and are swiped with the finger (1:1, 260 ms to settle). The swipe has no end: after the last page the first comes in from the same side (only the page dots jump back), and the other way round; a single page does not move. The list, the order, each page's look and what it shows come from the PanelOS integration (`pages` in its options); without it: home, weather, music, timers. Kinds: home (the weather page), weather, clock, music, timers, thermostat, quick controls, cameras, calendar, to-do, notifications. A kind may be on the list more than once: each page shows its own look, and every weather page has its own background and widgets. The weather details page is such a page too: a background (or the first custom page's) and weather widgets on the same 4 × 4 grid (by default the weather now, the details, the hourly and the daily forecast), whose widgets open nothing; the panel's own edit mode offers it the weather widgets only. A home widget opens its page; a page the list lacks slides in next to the current one without a dot and goes once left.
- **Over every page**: the status bar, the timer island, the page dots, the quick shade (a swipe down that starts on the status bar; it follows the finger), the voice view, new notifications (the window, or a strip under the status bar, as set in the integration), status screens and critical alerts, the screensaver.
- **Status bar**: every part has its fixed place on every page. At the left the hint (the connection problem first, then the muted microphone's line, then the wake word), in the middle one group of marks, always centred and always in this order: microphone off, speaker off, timers, the bell (with the count; with do not disturb the bell sleeps, also without notifications). A mark comes and goes with a small jelly; a tap on it turns the microphone or the speaker back on (the speaker at the level it had before), opens the timers page or the notifications. A page's own chip at the right is 152 px wide, a longer name cut.
- **Timer island**: the running timers in one capsule under the status bar, the nearest first, each with a ring in its colour (red in its last minute, pause bars when paused), then "+N". A tap on a timer opens its sheet on the timers page, "+N" the page. A flick up flies it into the status bar's timer mark; it comes back by itself when a new timer starts or one enters its last minute. It stays under the shade and the voice view and is not shown on the timers page.
- **New notifications**: a window in the middle under the status bar, over a dimmed page, in the notifications page's look (a card, a sticky note slapped on, a pager that drops in and buzzes, a TV news flash). Dismiss removes the notification (in Home Assistant too), OK closes the window and marks it read, a tap beside it works as OK. Several come into one window ("1 of 3"): a sideways swipe or the pager's arrows step, OK steps to the next. It stays for the integration's seconds (a finger on it holds the time; at the end it closes and the notification stays unread), or, with "until answered", until OK or Dismiss. In do not disturb, on the screensaver or under an alert strip only the bell's count changes.
- **Status screens and critical alerts**: the integration watches the house-wide rules and tells the panels. A status screen (a room in use, someone on a call …) covers the panel while its state lasts, with the panel's own words for its category unless the integration gives its own; a swipe or its button folds it into a strip in its colour right under the status bar (the bar stays visible) on every page: a tap on the strip opens the screen again, its × closes the strip (the state goes on without it; the timer island moves down under the strip, and the strip steps aside while a page's menu is open); one with a countdown ends by itself. A critical alert (smoke, CO, gas, water) takes the whole screen in plain red on the notifications page, whatever its look, and sounds until it is held for two seconds (when the integration allows holding); it then stays in a red strip, without ×, until its sensor clears. While they are active, the notifications page lists them above its notifications (see below), also a status screen whose strip was closed.
- **Notifications page**: the list, unread first, in one of four looks (plain, fridge notes, a pager, TV news). Above the notifications stand the active ones of the integration's alerts and status screens, the critical alert first (once held, in red), then the status screens, the newest first, each with its own words, picture and colour: a row in its colour in the plain look, a magnetic sign on the fridge, a "■" line at the top of the pager's list ("!" for the critical alert), a NOW headline in its colour on the TV news (ALERT for the critical alert). A tap on a status screen (on the pager: OK while it is the top line) opens it again, on top of the others, as a tap on its strip does; folded again, it has its strip back. "Clear all" and Dismiss leave them: they go when their state ends. The critical alert's row only shows it. In the plain look a tapped row opens the notification window's card (without its time line) over a dimmed page: it grows out of the row and shrinks back into it when a button or a tap beside it closes it. The other looks open one in their own view (the note to the front, the pager's LCD message, the big story). The settings (from the quick shade's page row) are the look's own menu: a card in the plain look, a magnetic whiteboard on the fridge, the pager's own LCD menu (▲ ▼ move the cursor, OK or a tap on a line changes it; the finish and the time format apply at once, a new look when the menu is left), the TV's on-screen menu. Each has the look and the panel's time format (the pager also its finish, the same value as the integration's); a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's menu. The menu and the open notification close when the page leaves the screen. The pager's and the TV news clock follow the time format.
- **Do not disturb**: from the quick shade, or set in the integration (on, or between two times). Critical alerts always come through; alarms, timers, status screens, the doorbell, voice answers and announcements by default (each can be turned off in the integration: alarms and timers then ring silently, a status screen comes as its strip only); new notifications go silently into the list.
- **Quick shade**: in two steps that scroll as one. The first pull shows brightness with a small Auto box (the light sensor sets the level; moving the slider unticks it), the four tiles as a row of icons and a chevron; pulling on (or the chevron) opens the second step: the title and the PanelOS mark, brightness, volume (set when the finger lifts, with a chime at the new level), the tiles with their names (microphone, theme Auto → Dark → Light, do not disturb, screen off) and, when the page on screen has settings, its page row ("Music settings" over the look's name, with the look's icon): a tap closes the shade and the page opens its settings in its look's menu.. Under both: what runs now, a card per timer (+1 min, pause, stop) and the newest notifications (a sideways swipe dismisses one, a tap opens it in the window). At the top of its content a pull down is the next step, at the end a push up the step before, then it closes; every pull starts again at the first step. Holding the PanelOS mark for a second opens the settings list; every row opens its page (the back arrow returns to the list):
  - **Wi-Fi**: the network list (strongest first, the saved ones marked), its password screen and a hidden network by name. A new network is tried before it is kept, so a wrong password or a network without Home Assistant never locks the panel out (see *Changing the Wi-Fi network* below).
  - **Home Assistant**: the setup screen with the address in use; a new address where Home Assistant answers is saved (the other settings in `app.json` stay) and the app starts again with it (another Home Assistant asks for the pairing again). The same address only says so.
  - **Display**: theme (the shade's three-way switch), brightness with its Auto box, time format (24-hour / 12-hour), screensaver after and style (a clock or the screen off). The same settings as the integration's Display card: a change on either side shows on the other.
  - **Voice**: the microphone, the wake word and a second one (or none), named as the voice satellite's models name them.
  - **Sound**: the volume (a chime at the new level when the finger lifts).
  - **Storage**: per partition, read on the panel when the page opens: the PanelOS slot from its boot image header, the data partitions with their biggest folders, what is not used yet.
  - **Touch** (the calibration), **About** (version, model, the panel's and Home Assistant's addresses, the project page and the issue tracker as QR codes: a tap on a link shows its code), **Restart**.
- **Touch calibration** (settings list, or from the integration): nine points to touch; a touch counts only within 40 px of the point shown (a tap elsewhere is not a measurement: the line asks for the point itself). Cancel leaves it at any time without changing anything; started from the settings list it goes back there, also when it is done. The panel corrects the touch positions only when the offsets are systematic (all the same way, more than 3 px on average, spread under 6 px), stores the correction and says what it did. A touch device that disappears (its driver bound again) is let go and looked for again every 5 s, without restarting the app.
- **Music page**: the record on the turntable has the cover's own colour as it is, black, white and grey too (the strongest hue of a 32-pixel copy, weighted by how colourful and common it is; for a grey cover its mean tone). The page's accent (buttons, bars) is the same hue brought to a readable brightness (a grey cover's own tone; the panel's accent without a cover); the tone arm keeps the brand colour. It crossfades with the cover (400 ms). The platter and the cassette reels spin up and coast down with a speed that never jumps: a start while it still coasts, or a stop while it spins up, goes on from the speed it has.
- **Quick controls page** (tiles, mixing desk, flight deck): a tap toggles; a long press on a tile, on a channel's tape under its fader or on a lever's plate opens the control's details in the look's menu: the tile grows into a card, the channel's rack unit slides down into the rack above the desk and its keys light up left to right, the switch's overhead sub-panel swings down from its hinge and rattles to a stop. They hold what the control can do: on / off, its level with its unit (brightness, position, set temperature), colour temperature, colours, the effect, a cover's open / stop / close (the key of the way it moves lit); a value set stays where it was let go until the device follows (at most 3 s). The page's settings (the quick shade's page row) hold the look; a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's. The desk's tapes are as wide as their strips (8 channels fill the desk, fewer get up to 84 px) and take a long name in smaller letters or in two lines, the deck's plates the same. The flight deck paints group frames over its levers: automatic (neighbours of one kind: lights, shutters, climate, switches, media …), off (one frame over the top row with the panel's area) or the user's own groups; a frame stops at the end of a row and the next row starts a new one with the same name. A colour light's lamp lights in the light's own colour at the throw: its last colour is kept, also over a restart (`quick-colours.json` beside the settings); a light the panel has never seen on lights when the light reports back.
- **Clock faces** (the screensaver, the clock page and the clock widget, `panels.slint`, `home.slint`, `clocks.slint`): digital, big digits, round, square, minimal, numerals, wandering, split-flap (the widget: the time in words instead of wandering), and three drawn ones: nixie tubes on a walnut plinth (smoky glass, so a background shows through; seconds optional), an LCD quartz watch (its resin case from 3 × 2 cells, the LCD alone below that; a tap lights it for 3 s; AL when an alarm is on) and a red twin-bell clock (its alarm hand at the next alarm; it rings with an alarm). The drawn ones keep their own proportions and are scaled to fit, centred. Smallest widget sizes: nixie 3 × 1 (4 × 1 with seconds), LCD 2 × 1, twin-bell 2 × 2.
- **Timers tab** (`timers.slint`, its menus in `timer_menus.slint`, the arcade screen in `arcade.slint`): five looks, plain, kitchen, hourglass, synthwave and flip. The plain and synthwave looks lay three or more timers out in a grid of four cells: with three the fourth is a "+", with more the grid shows the four that end soonest (running ones by the time left, then paused ones), each cell keeping its place while its set stays. Kitchen, hourglass and flip show one timer big with a chip for every timer (equally wide, wrapping onto more rows) and the round + whenever timers run; plain and synthwave show the round + with one or two timers. A tap on a timer in the plain or synthwave grid opens its menu over its cell (the name, a ring with the time counting down, +1 min, pause / go on, cancel); the "+" and the quick starts' custom button open the new timer (hour and minute drums that follow the finger and glide one row per step, on the flip look split-flap cards: a tap on a card's lower half adds one, on its upper half takes one; a label line; Start); the quick shade's page row opens the page's settings (functions, colours, the timer look and, for synthwave, its scene). Each menu is an object of the look: a card that grows in, a recipe card dropped onto the fridge with a magnet, a parchment scroll unrolling between two wooden rods, an arcade screen that switches on, a split-flap board whose lower half folds down; on the arcade screen every choice is a block and everything moves in whole 40 ms frames. A new look closes the menu and opens it again in the new look; menus close when the page leaves the screen, the screensaver comes or something rings. Whenever the tab comes into view, every timer runs from zero to where it stands in its look's way (rings fill and times count up with rolling digits, the kitchen scale winds up with a ratchet, the hourglass turns over and pours to its level, the synthwave tally counts in eight steps, the flip cards flap up from 00:00); out of view the timers wait at zero. A timer's time rolls its changed digits as it counts down. Labels take up to 60 characters (alarms 32): the keyboard view counts them and shakes the field when a key is pressed at the limit, and the end of a long label stays in view.
- **Stopwatch and alarm looks** (timers page options, besides the timers' own look): a chronograph, a timing tower, an LCD sports watch and nixie tubes for the stopwatch; a twin-bell clock, a sunrise sky, a clock radio and a 24-hour dial for the alarms. The tabs, the page dots and the buttons stay where the plain look has them.
  - **Alarms in every look**: the + (top right) opens a new alarm, except on the clock radio, whose three preset keys are the alarms (AL1-AL3 lit while on; an empty key is a "+" for a new alarm; the radio handles three). The plain look lists the alarms with switches (a tap on a row edits it); the twin-bell, sunrise and 24-hour dial show them as chips, two to a row, further rows scroll. A tap on a chip or a preset key switches that alarm on or off, a hold of half a second opens it in the editor (its dot or LED blinks twice while held, it opens on release). The radio sits in the middle of the free space under the tabs and has a 12H / 24H slide switch on its body.
  - **The alarm editor** comes down under the status bar as an object of the alarm look (`alarm_menus.slint`): the brand card, the twin-bell's enamel face (it drops in and rings), the dawn sky (it rises from its lower edge while a warm glow comes up), the clock radio's plastic with its LED and HOUR / MIN keys and day keys with LEDs (its LED powers up after it lands), the night dial with a 24-hour ruler whose sleep arc follows the drums. Time (drums that glide a row in 180 ms; in 12-hour mode hours 1-12 and an AM / PM drum), days, sound (a designed sound plays once as it is picked), label (the keyboard), Save, and Delete for a saved alarm. A tap beside it closes it without saving.
  - **Each alarm look rings its own way**: the twin-bell clock shakes with sound strokes beside its bells, the sunrise fills the page with a sky whose sun rises over 20 s, the clock radio blinks the alarm time with its snooze bar in Signal and sound rings from the grille, the 24-hour dial points its hand at the alarm with the marker pulsing; the plain look keeps the brand ringing screen. Snooze 9 min sits in the same place in every look. A snoozed alarm shows in the next-alarm line of every look ("Snoozed · rings at 6:54") until it rings again.
  - **Time format**: every alarm time (rows, chips, keys, the next-alarm lines, the editor, the ringing screens) follows the panel's 24-hour / 12-hour value (`Clock.h24`); the analog faces show no option.
  - **Laps** (the flag while it runs): every stopwatch look lists them, newest on top, older ones scroll into view: plain three rows, the chronograph moves its dial left and lists them beside it, the timing tower five rows, the LCD two LCD lines, the nixie two lines.
  - **Entrances**: whenever the alarm or the stopwatch tab comes into view (the pages settle on the timers page with it open, it is picked, the screensaver or a ring ends), its look runs once from its zero to where it stands; out of view it waits at its zero, and data updates do not start it again. Alarms, in at most 0.7 s: the plain look's next-alarm line counts its minutes down from a full day to the time left; the twin-bell's hands spin forward from 12:00 and the hammer taps a bell on arrival; the sunrise sky brightens from night to the sky of now while the big time rolls up from 0:00 (in the panel's time format); the clock radio's LED powers up (every segment at 45 % for 80 ms, then the time) and the preset LEDs of the alarms that are on light one after the other; the 24-hour dial draws its sleep arc from now to the alarm while the time left counts up. Stopwatch, in at most 0.6 s: the time counts up from zero and goes on live (plain, with the laps rolling in from the top; the chronograph, whose hands sweep forward, one turn at most, while its lap markers drop onto the bezel one after another; the timing tower, whose total and lap time count up while its rows drop in); the LCD watch lights every segment for 80 ms, then shows the time with its lag; the nixie tubes run through every digit once and stop on theirs.
  - **Settings** of the alarm and stopwatch tabs come from the quick shade's page row ("Timers settings · Alarms · Clock radio") in the look's own menu (`alarm_menus.slint`, `watch_menus.slint`): functions, colours, the time format (alarm looks with digits) and the look; the stopwatch's in the brand card, the steel case back (a glint sweeps across when it seats), the timing board (its rows slide in), the LCD watch (pressing LIGHT: the LCD lights teal and fades back) and a walnut base with a brass plate (the amber writing warms up like the tubes). A look change reopens the menu as the new look's. The menus and the editor close when the page leaves the screen, the tab changes or a ring starts.
- **Each look moves in its own way**: besides the shared motions (sheets, digits, marks), every look has a motion of its own: a clock face's changing date or digit, the nixie tubes' afterglow and their cycle through every digit after a reset, the LCDs' lagging segments, the twin-bell's hammer tapping a bell when an alarm is switched on, the hourglass turning over for a new timer, the turntable's arm shadow, the receipt jerking up as a thermal printer feeds, a new to-do item typed, printed or written in, the departure board blinking NOW, a new notification sliding, slapping or buzzing in, the flight deck's lamp test. They are drawn only while they run.
- **Synthwave scenes** (home background, timers, thermostat): mountains, city, beach and space (a ringed planet in a nebula over cyan wireframe mountains, its light changing from day to night, shooting stars at any time). The cars drive along the nine middle floor lines (−4 … +4; the outer ones come in from a lower corner). The panel shows one scene on its home, weather and thermostat pages: the first page with a synthwave background chooses it. The timers page has its own scene, or a random one: a new one, never the same twice in a row, is picked while the page is out of sight (a second or two after it went, with no finger on the pages), so the page brings it from its first frame however it is opened (a swipe, the island, the status bar's mark, a ring) and it never changes on screen.
- **Taps on the home background**: on the synthwave scene a tap on the sky sends a shooting star, a tap on the road asks for a car on the floor line under the finger (one every 3 s at most), a tap on a car shoves it (it yaws away, slides a lane or so sideways in tyre smoke, slows down, then steers back; the patrol car keeps its light bar going). On the drawn scenes a tap lights or darkens the city window under the finger (the street leaves it so for a minute) or shakes the trees, and the flag flutters harder for a while.
- **Rain on the glass** (`src/glass.rs`, `src/glass_gl.rs`): over the photo backgrounds (the weather pages' photos and the weather details' living photo), while it rains, drops land on the glass. They sit, merge, and the heavy ones run down, stretched, leaving small beads and a fading wet streak; the wind, with its gusts, bends their path, and when the rain stops they dry up. How many follow the scene's rain (drizzle, rain, pouring; a thunderstorm pours). Each drop is a small lens: it shows an inverted, wider view of what is under it, text and widgets included. The drops lie on the glass, not on the page: a swipe to the next photo page keeps them, and they fade out with a page without a photo, under the screensaver, the voice view, the shade, a sheet or a status screen; back after more than 20 s, the glass is as wet as the rain has made it by then. A stroke up or down (or slanted more steeply than 50°) wipes them away along the finger: what the finger pushes aside stays as small beads at the edges of its path, the water it gathered stays as one drop where it leaves the glass (a big one runs down), and new drops come with the rain. A sideways stroke still turns the page, and the shade still comes only from the status bar. The drops are simulated on the CPU and drawn by the GPU after Skia's frame: the frame is copied at half size into a texture, and every drop, bead and streak is one small instanced quad whose shader looks through it at that copy (a streak only darkens what is under it). A dry glass, or one off screen, draws nothing and asks for no frame. Both are switches of the panel's device in Home Assistant, on by default: **Rain on the glass**, and **Lightning** for the storm scenes' flashes and bolts.
- **Notifications** keep the time they first came: Home Assistant makes some again with a new time at every start (an invalid config), so the panel remembers each one by its title and text (`notes-seen.json` beside the settings, forgotten a day after it went).
- **Sheets** follow the finger on their handle and header: a short one (it fits under the status bar) has one height; a tall one (a long room, player or day list) opens at medium height and goes up to just under the status bar, where its list scrolls; flicks and distances change the height or close it, and the scrim follows.
- **Editing a custom page on the panel** (`home_edit.rs`, `HomeGrid` in `home.slint`; only with the integration's "Allow editing on the panel"): the app watches the finger before the interface gets it (`platform.rs` → `home_edit::finger`); a finger that rests 0.7 s on a custom page with nothing over it (`home-hold-ok`) lets go of what it pressed (so lifting it taps nothing) and opens the lock (a title and a line, different each time); holding the lock fills its ring in 1 s and opens the edit mode. A drag moves a widget (its landing cells outlined), the thick corner resizes it (never below its smallest size), − removes it, a tap opens its settings menu (spec 42.3: the widget stays lit in its cell as the live preview, the card unfolds under or over it), + opens "Add widget" (42.2: live previews; a pick lights the free cells, a tap on one puts it there) and the palette opens the synthwave background's arcade screen (42.1, `arcade.slint`). Each change goes into the panel's copy of the settings (the page shows it at once) and to the integration as the page's widget list (`panelos/page_set` with `items`); a copy of the settings coming back before the change is in them does not undo it. Leaving the page, the screensaver or Done ends the edit mode. A People widget's menu lists the people the panel knows as checkboxes (all ticked: everyone; the last one ticked stays) and keeps the choice as the widget's `people`.
- **Menus** (`MenuHost` in `widgets.slint`): every picker and page settings menu comes out of what opened it, as an object of the look (a record sleeve, a J-card, an arcade screen …). The host places it (a picker hangs from the chip, right edge at x 464 from y 48; page settings come down under the status bar, x 16–464), lets it grow to its content up to y 468 (beyond that the look scrolls its body), dims the page to 30 % and closes it on a tap beside it. The look draws the menu and its motion from the host's clocks (`t-in` / `t-out` in ms, `t-in-40` / `t-out-40` for the looks that step in whole 40 ms frames); the clocks run only while a motion lasts, so a menu at rest draws nothing. The shared controls take the look's colours as tokens (`tk-raised`, `tk-line`, `tk-text`, `tk-muted`, `tk-on-text`, `tk-menu`, `tk-pressed`, `tk-track`, `tk-knob`, `tk-accent`; the defaults are the plain look's). The quick shade's page row opens the settings of the page on screen in its look's menu (`PageMenu`).
- **The arcade screen** (`arcade.slint`): the neon looks' menus come up on a small CRT in the scene's colour (glass with scanlines, a tube border with its glow, a chrome title; it switches on as a line that opens under a flash and switches off to a line and a dot, at 30 frames a second). Choices on it are arcade blocks: words in the pixel font, the current one an inverse block that blinks twice when picked; everything on it moves in whole 40 ms frames.
- **Thermostat menus** (`thermo_menus.slint`): the device chip opens the rooms, the option pills the options (dial and neon; the remote's keys and the wall's slide switches step them in place), the quick shade's page row the settings. Dial: the brand card. Remote control: a zone LCD in the remote's plastic, the settings on its sliding lid. Wall thermostat: the zone plate with a pilot lamp per zone, the settings behind its hinged front door. Neon: the arcade screen. A tap on a room shows its device; a look picked in the settings closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's. The remote's LCD clock follows the panel's time format; a tap on it switches the format (12H / 24H printed under it). The menus close when the page leaves the screen.
- **Rotary controls**: the thermostat's dial ring (dial and neon looks) and the wall look's knob turn with the finger, relatively, to the device's steps; the target is sent when the finger lifts. A drag that starts on the ring or the knob never turns the page: the page tells the pager where its ring is (`Act.lock-*`), and the pager checks the press against it before its own 100 ms hold ends. A swipe that starts in the middle of the dial or outside the ring turns the page.
- **Calendar views** (`calendar.slint`, `cal.rs`): the plain look's day, 3-day and week views are time grids. Whenever the page comes on screen (a swipe, the screensaver gone) and whenever the view changes, the grid shows the period with today in it (‹ › browse from there: a day, three days or a week at a time; the 3-day view is today and the next two days) and scrolls so the current time is in the middle of the grid, as far as the day allows. An hour is 44 px in the day view; the 3-day and week views fit 08:00–18:00 on their grid. Two fingers moving apart or together vertically make the hours taller or shorter round the hour under the fingers, from the whole day to four hours on the grid; one finger scrolls as before, and the zoom stays until the page comes on screen again or the view changes. Two fingers reach Slint like this: `touch.rs` reads the driver's multitouch slots next to its single-touch emulation; while two contacts are down the pointer stops (the first finger's press is let go with a pointer exit, so it taps and drags nothing), and `platform.rs` sends Slint pinch events (`ScaleRotateGestureHandler` over the grid) once the fingers' vertical spread has changed by 8 px; the finger left after a pinch does nothing until it lifts. An event's description and place are shown readable: HTML tags go (line breaks, paragraphs and list items start lines), entities are read, link targets in angle brackets go, a long bare link shows only its host, lines without letters or digits (separators) go, blank runs become one empty line, and a description ends with "…" after 1500 characters. Every wrapped text in the calendar's menus has `min-width: 0`: a wrapped text's narrowest width is its longest word, and one long link would widen the whole card and push its rows out of sight.
- **Calendar menus** (`cal_menus.slint`, spec 37): four menus in every look. The chip's picker shows or hides the page's calendars (at least one stays on; the last one shakes; the integration keeps the choice as `hidden_calendars`; the chip reads "4 calendars" or the one name) and, in the plain look, picks the view (kept as the last view, the start view after a restart). The settings (the quick shade's page row) hold the look and the time format. A day's list comes from a month day, a week or 3-day column, a wall calendar day or the tear-off's "+n more"; the departure board's head shows the week's departures. An event's details sit over a list (a tap beside them goes back to it); Edit, for calendars that allow it, turns them into the edit form in the same object: the title on the keyboard, ‹ › a day, all day, and the start and end on the time drums in place (moving the start keeps the length; the end never goes before the start). Save writes the event with `calendar/event/update`; Delete hides it at once and sends `calendar/event/delete` when its undo strip has gone (5 s). Plain is the brand card; the departure board has flap boards hanging on two rods (their cells flip in), the station's printed timetable (it rolls down) and a boarding pass (printed in three jerks); the tear-off has slips from the pad (they fall with a sway), its cardboard back (it swings down) and an appointment card; the wall calendar has ruled index cards pinned on beside the tapped day. Every calendar time follows the time format (on the departure board's flap cells a 12-hour time reads "6:45P").
- **Time format**: one panel-wide value, 24-hour (the default) or 12-hour, for every clock time; durations never change. It is kept in `settings.json` (`time_24h`) and set in three places, each showing the others' changes: the integration's Time format select (its Display card), the panel's settings → Display, and a look's own 12H / 24H key (`Act` `set.h24` with `"true"` / `"false"`). The UI reads `Clock.h24`; the app writes its clock times with `clock::hm(h, m)` / `clock::local_hm(t)` ("18:45" / "6:45 PM"), an hour alone with `clock::hour_label(h)` ("18" / "6 PM"), and `clock::parse_hm` reads either form back. Digital clock faces show the hour without a leading zero in 12-hour mode and a small AM / PM after the minutes on the digits' baseline (`ClockData.th` and `ampm`; `hh` stays 24-hour for the hands and dials); the split-flap clock a blank first card and an AM / PM flap, the nixie tubes a dark first tube and a small neon PM lamp, the LCD watch its PM segment above the seconds. Analog faces have no option; their texts follow it.
- **To-do page menus** (`todo_menus.slint`): the chip picks the list in the look's own object (the brand card, till paper fed out of a slot, the terminal's `todo --lists`, embossed tapes on a dark card, a page of the pad flipping down); the page settings (look, time format) open from the quick shade's page row in the same object. The terminal's config applies a new look when its menu is left; elsewhere a new look closes the menu and opens it again as the new look's menu. The label maker's menu tapes come from the app's own tape drawer (`Job::TapeLabel`: a long name goes onto more tapes, the shown list is marked ▶ and rimmed). The shared controls take the look's shape and letters as tokens too (`tk-radius`, `tk-font`, `tk-font-size`, `tk-font-weight`, `tk-caps`; the segmented control's pill `tk-pill`). A new item lands at the top of its list and opens in 250 ms (lists that can move items: `todo/item/move` without `previous_uid`); a ticked item stays struck through for 1.5 s, then folds away in 250 ms and moves under Completed.
- **Keyboard** (to-do, alarm label, Wi-Fi password): a 0.4 s press on a letter shows its accented forms (Hungarian first), the finger slides to one and letting go types it; the globe key switches Latin / Cyrillic letters; an empty field starts with a capital, also after deleting back to the start.
- **Taps** reach the app through one callback (`Act` in `theme.slint`): what (`music.play`, `quick.toggle`, `set.brightness` …) and a value. Each page has its own module: `shade.rs`, `quick.rs`, `thermo.rs`, `cam.rs`, `cal.rs`, `todo.rs`, `info.rs`, `mus.rs` (music players), `synth.rs` (the synthwave scenes); clock, weather, home widgets, music and timers in `pages.rs` and `main.rs`.
- **Data**: live states through the Home Assistant connection (`subscribe_entities` for exactly the entities the pages show); calendars and to-do items over Home Assistant's REST API in threads (every 5 min / every minute and after a change); camera frames through the camera proxy only while the cameras page is on screen; notifications through `persistent_notification/subscribe`; the music players for the picker through a template, so the panel never loads the whole state list.
- **Smooth by construction**: values that change every second (seconds hands, the music position, the stopwatch) move as UI animations from Slint's animation clock; the app sets a page's data only when it changed and updates rows of a list in place, so nothing is built again or laid out anew while it is on screen; static widget parts are drawn once into layers; pictures arrive as RGBA prepared in the thread that made them. Pictures of the screen (Home Assistant's live preview, screenshots) are read from the buffer on screen through its dma-buf in a thread, never back from the GPU inside a frame. At start, under the start-up picture, and again after the page list changed (under the page shown), every page is drawn once, so the first swipe to it does not have to build it. Text-heavy looks that only change once a minute (the calendar's) are drawn into a layer, so a swipe only moves a picture. A still view stands still: only the breathing colon, the second hands and the seconds line redraw (30 times a second), and only while their page is on screen (with a part of it during a swipe) and the screensaver is off; clocks, the nixie glow and the news crawls of the pages out of sight do not run. The synthwave scenes' cars and palms are planned once a second only while a scene is on screen: the scene time comes from Slint's animation clock, and every read of `animation-tick()` asks for a frame, so the app reads it only then. A binding that uses an animated value twice behind a condition (`a && f(x) && g(x)`) is split into two properties: the Slint compiler computes a twice-used operand before the condition, so the animated value is read in every frame anyway (the wandering clock face's corner spark kept every clock face at 60 frames a second). **Calm clocks**: when nobody has been near (proximity sensor) and nothing was asked of the panel (a touch, a Home Assistant request: settings, views, commands, notifications, the screen; not the live preview's screenshots) for 10 minutes, every clock (widget, clock page, screensaver) turns calm: gradually over 5 s the breathing colon becomes a plain step, bright one second and dim the next, and the seconds tick once a second on the whole second, so a clock draws one frame a second instead of 30; the nixie tubes glow steadily. On a whole second the breath is at its top or bottom, so calm and breathing meet without a jump. Anything that stirs the panel brings the breathing back, gradually over 2 s (the app looks every 0.5 s, taps and requests at once). Texts that a parent re-evaluates every second (a running timer's row) take their new value only when it really differs, so their layers stay; a running time in neon is drawn digit by digit, each digit its own layer.
- **Pictures drawn by the app** (`src/draw.rs`): the label maker's tapes, the notepad's handwriting, the fridge notes, the wall calendar's pen notes and the mixing desk's labels: tilted strips with uneven ends and raised letters, letters that each sit a little differently, pen strokes with a wobble. Skia draws them on the CPU in a thread of their own, random but stable for each item (seeded by its text), and the page shows each as soon as it is ready; drawn ones are kept.
- **Texts**: the interface's word lists (titles, empty-state lines, hints, the voice view's lines) live in `src/text.pak`; a page keeps its lines while it is on screen and gets new ones for its next visit.
- **Assets**: pictures and fonts live in the UI bundle (`/opt/panelos-ui/share/ui`) and are read at run time by their path there; the image keeps a small fallback app for panels without the bundle.
- **Brand**: the PanelOS palette and type in `theme.slint`; Signal marks lit things only (dots, an active state, one highlighted number), never a text ground; button text is Ink or Paper.

## Pairing, theme, brightness, approach

- **Pairing by QR code** (no token typing): on first start the panel shows a QR code with Home Assistant's normal login page (OAuth authorization-code flow, the panel is the IndieAuth client: `client_id` `http://<panel-ip>:8080/`, `redirect_uri` `http://<panel-ip>:8080/auth/callback`; Home Assistant accepts local IP client IDs). After the login on the phone, Home Assistant redirects the phone to the panel, which exchanges the one-time code for a refresh token. The token is stored on the panel only (`/mnt/persist/nspanel/ha_auth.json`, root-only); it can be revoked in the Home Assistant user profile, after which the panel shows the QR code again.
- **Theme**: dark, light, or auto (the default, from the first start, setup and pairing screens included). What switches Auto is set in the integration (`theme_auto`): the light in the room (the default), the sun, or set times. By the light the sensor decides, dark below 10 lx, light above 30 lx, unchanged in between, and a change only after the light stayed on the other side for 10 s; the first decision is immediate, before the first frame. By the sun it is dark while Home Assistant's `sun.sun` is below the horizon; by set times dark from one time to the other (20:00 to 7:00 by default, across midnight too). Without what the trigger needs (no sun state yet, the clock not set) the light sensor decides, and without a sensor reading the sun, otherwise dark. A switch by someone crossfades the whole screen in 0.7 s; a change Auto makes by itself blends over 3 s.
- **Brightness**: one scale for automatic and manual brightness, even in perceived brightness (gamma 2.2), from 0.8 % (still readable at night, no flicker) to 100 %. Automatic: STK3x3x ambient light, 1 lx and darker 0.8 %, 10 lx 16 %, 50 lx 41 %, 150 lx 66 %, 500 lx and brighter 100 %; the sensor's scale is switched automatically because it saturates at about 6500 lx on its most sensitive scale. Manual: 1..100 % on the panel and in Home Assistant. Changes fade every 20 ms in perceived brightness (about 0.8 s down, 0.25 s up, 0.12 s when waking from the screensaver).
- **Approach wake**: the proximity sensor runs at its most sensitive gain, 11.84 ms integration time and the IR LED at full power (`hw.rs`). The app learns the resting level and its jitter (the mean change between consecutive resting readings) and reports an approach after two readings, half a second apart, above the resting level plus the signal of a hand at the wake distance (`PROXIMITY_WAKE_CM`, 40 cm, from the measured distance curve), or plus 5 times the jitter if that is more: noisy readings shorten the range instead of waking the panel. A slow approach does not raise the threshold, since it changes each reading only a little, and readings above half the threshold teach nothing (a hand held just below it would pull it up); whatever stays in front of the sensor for over a minute becomes the new resting level. The wake distance is set in the integration (`wake_distance`): near (30 cm), medium (40 cm, the default) or far (45 cm); presence uses the same threshold. **Calibration** (started from the integration): a black screen counts down 10 s to step back, then shows a still line while the panel takes 20 readings in 10 s (the pages under it are not drawn and nothing on the screen moves meanwhile: redraws can disturb the readings), then says how far the panel now wakes, to the nearest 5 cm. The resting level becomes the readings' median and the jitter the value the learning settles at for their consecutive changes (the largest tenth left out); with a reading above half the resulting threshold (something moved in front) nothing changes and the panel says so. A tap ends it at any time, nothing changed. Measurements and the test view: `docs/bringup.md`.
- **Child lock** (a switch of the panel's device in Home Assistant): no touch reaches the pages, the shade, the status bar or any layer. A tap shows the lock card (the card and hold ring of the panel editing's lock); holding the lock for 3 s unlocks the panel, and it locks again after a minute without a touch or when the screensaver starts. A tap outside the card or its X closes the card, and it closes by itself after 6 s. Voice, the approach wake and Home Assistant's requests work as usual; a tap on the screensaver wakes the screen; a ringing timer or alarm still stops with a tap, and while a critical alert is up the lock steps aside so it can be held. The touch test (from the integration) works over it. Switching the lock on closes the shade, the settings and any page sheet.
- Icons: Material Design Icons 7.4.47 (the set Home Assistant uses), `mdi:` names from the entity or Home Assistant-like defaults per domain.
- **Screensaver**: one of the clock faces (style from the integration), or screen off; wakes on tap (on release, so the tap does not reach the page) or on approach; "Screen off" in the quick shade turns the screen dark until the next touch or approach. It starts after the idle time only inside its window, set in the integration: when the room is dark (the default: below 3 lx from the panel's light sensor or a Home Assistant one; measured at the panel, a lamp-lit evening room reads 5–26 lx and a dark room about 1 lx), between two times, from sunset to sunrise, or any time. Outside its window it gives way to the pages again (in the dark: once it is clearly brighter). It never starts while a timer runs or a status screen is up.

## Dashboard mode: web view

- A secondary function: the panel is made for the assistant first. The web view runs within the limits of four Cortex-A35 cores and a Mali-G31; a light, simple dashboard made for the panel works best.
- With the web bundle mounted (`/opt/panelos-web`, see `buildroot/configs/panelos_web_defconfig`), dashboard mode shows the Home Assistant dashboard chosen in the integration in WPE WebKit 2.50 (Release build, JavaScript JIT up to FTL, Skia, Mesa Panfrost on the Mali-G31). `src/web.rs` starts the `panelos-web` helper chrooted in the bundle. Login like the Home Assistant apps (external auth), header and sidebar can be hidden.
- **Direct frames.** At start the app creates three more display buffers (`Display::create_web_buffers`) and hands them to the helper as dma-bufs (`BUFS`). While the web view is all there is on the screen (no settings, conversation, message, camera, weather, media, timer, screensaver or start-up layer; checked on every turn of the event loop), the helper draws each WebKit frame into a free one on the GPU and the display flips to it (`FRAMEBUF`, `FREE`); the UI does not draw at all. With anything on top the frames go through shared memory as images under the UI's layers, as before (`DIRECT 0` also hands over the frame on screen).
- **Loading screen.** WebKit's load start (`LOADING`) puts `loading.slint` over the web view: the mark writes itself in Morse on black, round after round. The helper checks the page every 300 ms through the shadow roots (Home Assistant's `hui-root`): ready (`READY`) when cards are drawn, the element count is the same three times in a row, icons have their drawing and images are in (icons waited for at most 20 s after the dashboard appeared, a view without cards 10 s), and no new frames came for 700 ms (or 4 s passed); at most 45 s. Then the screen fades in 450 ms. Measured on a dashboard of 15 cards: Home Assistant's frontend takes ~15 s to start, the cards come at 20–27 s. The integration option `loading_screen` turns it off (Home Assistant's own start-up shows instead).
- **Size.** The integration option `zoom` reaches the helper as `ZOOM` (WebKit's zoom level).
- **Pull to refresh.** A touch that starts in the top 24 px is the settings swipe and never reaches the page. Any other touch goes to the page, and at touch down the app asks the helper (`ATTOP x y`) whether the content under the finger is scrolled to its top (the nearest scrolling element through shadow roots and slots, or the page). If it is, a pull of 16 px straight down starts pull to refresh: a dark disc comes down with the finger and the mark writes itself in it; after 110 px more, letting go reloads the page (`RELOAD`, behind the loading screen). Scrolling up or moving sideways first is not a pull.
- **Fonts.** DejaVu Sans plus Noto Sans (package `noto-sans`, from Google Fonts at a fixed commit) as a wide fallback: cards use characters DejaVu and Roboto lack, e.g. Mushroom's separator U+2E31.
- **Skipped resources.** Home Assistant loads every card resource on every dashboard. The integration works out the ones the dashboard shown does not need (`resources.py`; the panel reports which dashboard it shows) and sends them with the settings; they reach the helper as `SKIP`; it blocks those URLs with a WebKit content filter, and the page load waits until the filter is ready.
- Measured on the panel (scroll test page with touches from the development input; the dashboard scrolled by a script, no touches):

  | | before | direct frames |
  |---|---|---|
  | frame to the app (read back / GPU copy) | ~30 ms | ~2.5–3.5 ms |
  | frame made → on screen | ~52 ms | ~13–21 ms |
  | frames per second while swiping (test page) | 14–25 | 38–56 |
  | dashboard, scripted scroll | 16–17 /s | 16–20 /s (limited by the page's own work) |

  First load of a dashboard with 35 card resources (8.9 MB of JavaScript): ~21 s with everything, ~15–17 s with the 33 it does not need skipped. Loading is limited by the CPU (scripts, style, layout); the GPU is busy at most 13 % of the time. Skia painting on the CPU (3 threads) instead of the GPU made no clear difference.
- Still on the page side: heavy card code and effects (blur behind a bar, many scripts) cost on four Cortex-A35 cores; the page stays loaded between uses (switching modes does not reload it).

## ESPHome native API

- The app is an ESPHome API server on TCP 6053 (`esphome.rs`; transport in `api_link.rs`): only Home Assistant's address may connect, and Home Assistant gives the panel a key after adding it and connects encrypted from then on (Noise, as ESPHome devices; details in [security.md](security.md)). Message types are generated from `app/proto/api.proto` (aioesphomeapi, MIT). Home Assistant's built-in ESPHome integration adds the panel by IP, or by discovery.
- mDNS (`app/src/mdns.rs`, no system daemon): the panel announces `nspanel-pro._esphomelib._tcp` (port 6053; TXT `friendly_name`, `version`, `mac`, `network`, `project_name`, `project_version`, the same values as in the API's device info), answers queries for it and for `nspanel-pro.local`, and announces again after an address change. Home Assistant matches the `mac` record with a configured device and only updates its address. mDNS stays inside one network segment unless the router repeats it.
- Entities on the ESPHome device (the voice satellite): Volume (number), Mute microphone (switch), Wake level, Wake SNR, Wake score (sensors; sent with every wake word before the voice run starts, so Home Assistant can let the satellite that heard best answer: level = RMS of the last second in dBFS, SNR = loudest 100 ms of the last second over the second before it in dB, score = the model's averaged probability), plus the voice satellite entities Home Assistant creates itself. The device is named "NSPanel Pro Voice", maker PanelOS, model Voice satellite.
- `src/esphome.rs`: framing, entity list, state diffing (changes pushed when the UI thread notifies), commands mapped to the same setting keys as the on-panel settings screen.

## The panel's device (PanelOS integration)

- The display, the settings and the sensors are entities of the PanelOS integration's device ("NSPanel Pro", maker PanelOS, model NSPanel Pro 86 Gen1), which the ESPHome device is connected via: Screen (switch: off = screensaver now), Dashboard (select, options = the dashboards the panel's user may open), Theme, Screensaver after, Screensaver style, Display mode, Time format (selects), Brightness (number), Automatic brightness, Wake on approach, Child lock (switches), Illuminance, SoC temperature, Wi-Fi signal, IP address, App version (sensors), Presence (binary sensor from the proximity sensor), Restart app, Reboot (buttons).
- `src/panel_device.rs` turns the device state into the report and the commands into settings; `src/ha.rs` sends `panelos/report` over the panel's Home Assistant connection once the integration has accepted its subscription (device info once, then only changed states and select options, checked every 200 ms) and passes the `command` events on. The protocol is in [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md#how-it-talks-to-the-panel).
- Recorded sensors are sent only after a set change (`report_device` in `main.rs`, steps in `settings.rs` `Reporting`, set in the integration's options): illuminance by a share of the last value sent (default 15 %, at least a third of it in lux), SoC temperature (1 °C) and Wi-Fi signal (3 dB) on five-minute averages of once-a-minute readings. Details in [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md#sensor-reporting).
- rtw88 does not fill `/proc/net/wireless`; the Wi-Fi signal comes from `iw dev wlan0 link`.

## Voice satellite

- The panel is an Assist satellite through the same ESPHome API connection (`VOICE_FEATURES` = voice assistant, speaker, API audio, timers, announcements; Home Assistant only offers the wake word selects when the announce flag is set). Home Assistant shows it as an assist satellite with Assistant / Assistant 2 and Wake word / Wake word 2 selects, like ESPHome voice satellites.
- **Wake words** run on the panel: microWakeWord models downloaded by `tools/build_image.sh` into `/usr/share/nspanel/wakewords`: `okay_nabu` (OHF-Voice release 20241226.3, cutoff 0.85), `hey_jarvis`, `hey_mycroft`, `alexa`, and the experimental `hey_home_assistant`, `okay_computer`, `choo_choo_homie`, `hey_peppa_pig`. The internal `stop` model is not offered in Home Assistant: it listens only while the panel speaks or a timer rings (and 2 s after), and stops it. Home Assistant's Wake word selects list what the panel reports; any microWakeWord model (`<id>.tflite` + `<id>.json`) in that folder appears there. Only enabled models run: the idle app takes 6.9 % of one core with two wake words. TensorFlow Lite (Buildroot package) and the TFLite micro frontend are compiled into the app (`app/native/`).
- **Wake word reset**: the streaming models keep their recent input inside the model (resource variables), so after a conversation the wake word that started it would fire again as soon as listening resumes. `mww_reset` feeds 3 s of silence features through every model.
- **Echo cancellation**: WebRTC AudioProcessing 1.3 (AEC3 + high-pass filter, Buildroot package `webrtc-audio-processing`, `app/native/aec.cc`), 16 kHz mono, 10 ms frames. The reference is the player's own output (written to ALSA in 10 ms pieces, 48 kHz → 16 kHz, ALSA buffer 100 ms). Measured: microphone level 15–28 dB lower during a spoken answer; the panel's own voice saying a wake word does not wake it. It runs only while the speaker plays and 2 s after (continuously it would cost about a quarter of a core). With it the wake word also works while the panel speaks: it cuts the answer off and starts a new conversation. Noise suppression is off (the wake word frontend has its own; Home Assistant's STT gets the echo-free voice).
- **Audio**: in from the PDM microphones (`hw:CARD=pdmmics`), 4 channels at 16 kHz, the mono signal is the mean of channels 2 and 3 with a fixed gain; out to `hw:CARD=rk809` at 48 kHz (chimes, TTS, timer beeps; 16 kHz, WAV and MP3 sources are resampled; MP3 decoded with `minimp3`).
- **Streamed answers**: Home Assistant sends the spoken answer over the API about as fast as it is spoken, with waits of up to about 0.7 s between chunks; the panel starts it once 0.8 s of audio is in (or after 1.5 s), and logs the chunk statistics, the longest wait (as the API receives the audio and as the voice thread does) and speaker underruns. The conversation stays on screen until the speech ends.
- **Flow**: wake word → `VoiceAssistantRequest` → audio over the API until Home Assistant's VAD ends the run → events (STT text, intent, TTS start/end) drive the conversation display. `continue_conversation` opens the microphone again for a follow-up. If nobody starts to speak within 5 s of a follow-up, the panel aborts the run (`VoiceAssistantRequest start=false`: Home Assistant cancels the pipeline, no answer); otherwise Home Assistant's segmenter would wait 15 s. Speech start is Home Assistant's STT-VAD-start event or the panel's own level detector (10 ms frames 12 dB over the room level for ~150 ms; the room level is followed while the panel is idle and silent), so a reply that begins just before the 5 s mark is not cut off. Home Assistant's API TTS stream can be empty (a WAV of unknown length), so the panel fetches the TTS URL from the TTS-end event itself (`/api/tts_proxy/<token>.wav`).
- **Timers** (Assist timer events): nearest timer in the top bar, full-screen alarm for at most a minute with the timers page's sound (set in the integration, else its look's own: Marimba, Mechanical bell, Glass chimes, Synth, Digital), looping until it is stopped (a tap anywhere stops it; its second button gives a timer one more minute and puts an alarm to sleep for 9 minutes, after which it rings again); alarms ring the same way with their own sound (Birds by default), a critical alert with its own (SOS by default) until it is held or clears. The sounds (`app/src/sounds.rs`): the panel's own in the UI bundle (`ui/sounds/*.wav`), uploaded ones fetched from Home Assistant into `/mnt/data/panelos-sounds` and kept as long as a setting uses them. An alarm with music asks Music Assistant (`music_assistant.play_media`) to play on the panel's own media player and rises from quiet to full over 30 s; without audio after 12 s its default sound rings. By voice: a wake word heard by the panel stops a ring at once; what someone says over the ring (from 8 dB over what is left of its echo) and every "Stop" go to the speech recognition, and only a stop word or a short stop phrase stops it, nothing goes to the assistant. When a timer starts ringing during a voice run, the run's sentence stops it only if the whole sentence is a stop phrase (stop words, with at most a few small words such as "már" or "the timer"); any other sentence, however short, goes on to the assistant. The ring shows on the timers page (a temporary one when the page list has none; leaving a page closes its sheets); when it stops or its minute is up, the panel goes back to the page it was on, unless someone paged meanwhile.
- **Voice view**: the full-screen conversation in the integration's look (plain, terminal, comic, oscilloscope). Each state reads apart in every look: in the oscilloscope "not understood" draws a violet question mark with the beam, an error a flat red line. The microphone level and the traces are measured only while the view shows (`voice::METER_ON`); closing the view (its time is up, or a tap) stops them.
- **Media player** (`app/src/media.rs`): an ESPHome media player entity (formats: WAV, 48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo, for music and announcements; pause, volume, mute, stop, play, announce). Home Assistant's media proxy converts any source to that WAV and the panel streams it (half a second buffered first) to the speaker; the volume is a share of the panel's volume. While it rings the panel listens itself, the ring playing on unchanged: a wake word stops it at once; a "Stop" from the model goes to the speech recognition with the 1.5 s before it (at a ring's start the echo canceller learns the new sound for about a second, and its leftover can sound like "Stop"); speech over the ring (the processed microphone signal 10 dB over what is left of the ring, its level over the last 2 s, for 60 ms) is cut out with half a second before it until 0.6 s of quiet (at most 3 s) and sent to the speech recognition of the panel's own assistant (`POST /api/stt/<engine>`, the engine and language from the integration), outside any conversation: only a short phrase with a stop word (állj, stop, elég, kapcsold ki …) stops the ring, and nothing goes to the assistant. The log notes the ring's leftover level, each piece of speech with its text, and why a ringing stopped (tap, voice, more time, a minute).
- **Announcements** (`assist_satellite.announce`) play and report back.
- **Sendspin player** (`app/src/sendspin.rs`): the panel is a player of Music Assistant through Sendspin, Music Assistant's own playback protocol (https://github.com/Sendspin/spec), so Music Assistant has it without any setup. The panel announces `_sendspin._tcp` over mDNS (port 8928, path `/sendspin`, name "NSPanel Pro") and Music Assistant connects to it. Every connection is end-to-end encrypted with Noise `KKpsk2_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256` (the panel is the responder); unpaired access is off. Pairing needs nobody: the panel reports its Sendspin client id among its ESPHome device capabilities (API 1.15, `DeviceCapabilitiesResponse`), Home Assistant asks its pairing token over the encrypted API (`SendspinPairingTokenRequest`, refused without the API key) and hands it to Music Assistant, which pairs with it (Pairing PSK flow) and connects with the long-term key from then on. The key pair, the pairing PSK and up to 8 pairing records are in `/mnt/persist/nspanel/sendspin.json` (0600). Audio: PCM 48 kHz 16-bit stereo chunks stamped with the server's clock; the spec's time filter (8 exchanges every 5 s, the best one fed in) maps them to the panel's clock, and the speaker thread mixes the music in when it is due (it measures the ALSA delay for each 10 ms piece; small errors are corrected by one frame per 10 ms, large ones by a jump), under every other sound and into the echo canceller's reference. While the assistant listens or thinks the music is silent; it plays on under the answer. Music Assistant's volume of the panel is the panel's own volume (both ways), mute is Music Assistant's own. A session that has not paired (the pairing PSK, or the sentinel PSK any host on the network gets) can only pair: it never holds the panel, never starts, clears or ends the music, its audio chunks are dropped and it never sets the music's clock. Only the paired server that holds the panel as a player feeds the time filter, and a new one starts it afresh (another server's clock base); a session that hears nothing for 30 s (the clock exchanges answer every 5 s) ends. Music Assistant shows the panel as one player of its device (with the Sendspin player as its output); the music page lists it first, as "This panel". When the app stops (the service stopping, or its restart button) it says goodbye with "restart" first, so Music Assistant connects again as soon as the app is back. Log: `sendspin: connected / activate / stream / playing, … ms buffered, music … dBFS`. Measured: paired 1 s after the first connection; 5 s buffered while playing.
- **Volume**: the quick shade's volume slider and the `Volume` number entity in Home Assistant (0–100 %, step 5, default 80 %). It sets the RK809 "Master Playback Volume": 100 % = 0 dB, −0.4 dB per percent, 0 % = off. A short chime plays at the new level.
- **Mute microphone**: switch in Home Assistant and on the panel (red icon in the top bar).
- Logging and testing: every pipeline event is logged (`Assist event <type> key=value…`, `voice: run end, follow-up yes/no`). `/tmp/nspanel-voice-test.wav` (16 kHz mono) is sent as the speech after the next wake word, `/tmp/nspanel-voice-followup.wav` as the speech of the next follow-up, so whole conversations can be replayed. `NSPANEL_AEC_DUMP=<file>` writes microphone and processed samples interleaved.

## Shown by Home Assistant

- **Integration actions**: `panelos.show_view` (a page: `home`, `weather`, `media`, `camera` with `entity_id`; `seconds`, 0 = until touched; it wakes the screen, keeps the screensaver away while it is up, and with seconds goes back to the page it was on when they run out, unless someone paged meanwhile; a timer ringing then keeps the panel until the ring ends; `app/src/visit.rs`) and `panelos.notify` (below).
- **PanelOS notifications**: the integration's `notify` entity per panel (`notify.send_message`) and the `panelos.notify` action (panels, title, message, icon, level, a picture from a camera or an address, up to three buttons, until answered) put a notification into the panels' lists and their windows, next to Home Assistant's persistent notifications. The integration keeps each one until it is answered (a panel that comes back gets the unread ones again); OK or a button marks it read on that panel, a button also fires `panelos_notification_action`, Dismiss removes it everywhere.
- **Requests from the integration's rules** (same path): `status` / `status_clear` (id, category, own headline / line / pictogram / colour, button, countdown), `critical` / `critical_clear` (id, title, message, icon, source, whether holding silences it), `touch_test` (starts the touch calibration).
- **Doorbell**: with a `ring` binary sensor on the cameras page, the panel wakes on that page when it rings, the picture big with a breathing ring, a chime (if do not disturb lets it through) and, with a `lock` on the page, "hold to open"; it goes after a minute or with ×.
- **Replies at the door**: with a speaker at the door on the cameras page (`speaker`, any media player; at first the one on a camera's own device), the visitor window shows a microphone. A tap grows a card of up to four replies out of it (`replies`, each one line; four defaults until set); a tap on one has Home Assistant say it on that speaker (`tts.speak` with the panel assistant's text-to-speech engine, in the page's `language` or the assistant's language and voice), the row lights and the card goes back into the microphone. The visitor window then stays another minute. No microphone without a speaker, replies or a text-to-speech engine.
- **Camera page settings**: from the quick shade's page row ("Camera settings · Cameras"), the brand card under the status bar: Picture (whole picture / fill the screen, kept by the integration) and Show (one camera / all cameras, as the chip); a change shows under the card at once.
- **Configuration** comes from the PanelOS integration in `custom_components/panelos` (see [home_assistant.md](home_assistant.md)): the page list (kind, look, entities, options per page), the weather and media player, the voice view's look, the dashboard for dashboard mode. The panel subscribes with `panelos/subscribe` and keeps the last configuration in `settings.json`, so a restart needs no reconnect. While the integration is missing the panel retries every minute, so installing it needs no panel restart.
- The voice assistant works in both display modes: full screen in assistant mode (unless switched off in the integration), a panel at the bottom in dashboard mode.

## Connection, start and outages

- **First screen with current content**: the Home Assistant client starts before the app takes the display over from the boot splash. The app waits until the clock is set and the first full state list is in (plus 300 ms for the assistant settings and forecasts); at most 25 s, at once when pairing is needed. Meanwhile the boot splash shows its light band.
- **Clock**: the panel has no battery-backed clock and starts in 1970. Until NTP has set it, the app takes the time from the `Date` header of Home Assistant's WebSocket handshake (`app/src/clock.rs`), which also works without internet access. Until either has set it, the UI shows `--:--` and no date.
- **NTP** (`S45ntp`): busybox `ntpd` starts as soon as Wi-Fi has an address, however late that is, and is started again 30 s after it exits.
- **Retries, for good** (a power cut can keep Home Assistant or the network away for hours): without network every 0.5 s during the first minute after the start, then every 5 s; while Home Assistant does not answer 2, 4, 8, then every 10 s. A vanished server is noticed within about 30 s (ping after 20 s without traffic, 10 s for the answer). A failure is logged once per cause, the recovery with the length of the outage.
- **Connection log** (`app/src/connlog.rs`): Home Assistant sessions ending (with the cause) and coming back, every roam check, move, link probe and link watch reconnect, one line each with the local time, in `/mnt/persist/nspanel/connection.log` (moved to `connection.log.1` at 128 KB, so the last ~256 KB stay). The system log lives in RAM, so a drop in the night can be looked at only here.
- **A short drop in Home Assistant**: the integration keeps the panel's entities (all but the restart and reboot buttons) with their last state for 30 s after the panel went away (a Wi-Fi hiccup, an app restart) and turns them unavailable only after that.
- **Status line**: "Waiting for the network…", "Home Assistant not reachable, retrying…", or Home Assistant's own error (login, dashboard).
- **Wi-Fi roam check** (`app/src/roam.rs`): from 5 minutes after the start, about every 30 minutes, and only while the panel is idle (no voice run, no awaited or playing answer, no announcement, no ringing timer, no Sendspin music; otherwise again a minute later). At -49 dBm or better there is no scan. Otherwise the panel scans for its own network by name (`wpa_cli scan ssid <hex>`, plain `wpa_cli scan` if that is refused; results after 8 s; an access point that hides the network name answers only such a scan) and moves with `wpa_cli roam` to the strongest access point of the same network if it is at least 10 dB stronger than the current one as the same scan saw it. The thresholds are those of ESPHome's `wifi.roam`. The move is one-shot: the network is never tied to one access point, so a panel moved to another room keeps working. Each check writes one line (`roam check: …`) with the access points of the network it saw, a move also the result 15 s later. Tested on the panel: moved by hand (`wpa_cli roam`) to an access point about 20 dB weaker, the first check moved it back; the re-association took about 1 s, and the Home Assistant connections stayed up through both moves. A strong signal is not always a good link: one access point lost most of its frames to the panel at full signal (AP→panel rate down to MCS 1–2, 20–66 % ping loss, 2 MB in 24–55 s), while a 17 dB weaker one on another channel carried the same 2 MB in 1.9 s without loss. So every 5 minutes while idle the panel also pings its router 20 times (0.2 s apart); two poor probes in a row (15 % or more lost, or 150 ms or more on average; no answer at all does not count, that is the link watch's case) make it avoid that access point for two hours and move to the best other one of the network (-75 dBm or better). Every move is probed the same way, and a poor new access point means a move back. The 30-minute check never picks an avoided access point; after two hours every access point counts again (`/tmp/panelos-poor-aps`, kept over app restarts until the next boot). Music counts as busy because a move drops the stream for a moment, so a panel that lost its access point during music and fell to a farther one stayed there as long as the music lasted, and the music broke up (the server's sends to the panel stalled for 5–8 s, the buffer ran dry). Now a stall (`sendspin: stall, …`: the music had played for 5 s, then the buffer stayed dry for a second; the gap after a start or a seek does not count) starts the check within 5 s during the music when nothing else is going on (`roam check: the music stalls, …`), also in the first 5 minutes after the start, at most every 10 minutes. The move still needs an access point at least 10 dB stronger, and at -49 dBm or better there is no scan. Development: `{"music_stall": true}` on the dev FIFO counts as a stall. Roaming can be switched off in the integration (`wifi_roaming`, on by default): then there are no roam checks, no stall checks and no link probes; the link watch (a link that carries nothing is made again) and the Wi-Fi power save staying off do not depend on it. The change is written to the connection log.
- Tested on the panel without switching anything off in the house (an `unreachable` route to Home Assistant, all routed traffic blocked except the test computer, a wrong Wi-Fi password in the RAM copy of the Wi-Fi config): Home Assistant gone for 75 s → noticed in 30 s, back 9 s after it returned; start without Home Assistant and internet → UI after 25 s with `--:--`, NTP set the clock 8 s after internet came back, Home Assistant connected 6–9 s after it returned; Wi-Fi gone for 90 s → noticed in 15 s, back in about 15 s.

- **Changing the Wi-Fi network** (`app/src/wifi.rs`, settings → Wi-Fi or the setup screen's Wi-Fi card): the new network is added to the running wpa_supplicant beside the current one (the passphrase turned into its key by `wpa_passphrase` on its standard input, WPA3-only networks with SAE) and selected. It counts as connected when the panel is associated (25 s at most), the DHCP client has given it an address after letting the old lease go (20 s) and Home Assistant answers at its address (three tries). Only then is it written to the saved config, `/mnt/persist/nspanel/wpa_supplicant.conf` (and its running copy), as the network of priority 1, with the one it replaces kept as a fallback of priority 0; the file is written whole to a temporary file, synced and renamed over the old one. Otherwise the panel selects the old network again, forgets the new one and says why (a wrong password, the network did not answer, no address, Home Assistant not answering there); the saved config is not touched. A saved network picked again is selected the same way. The roam check and the link watch wait while a switch runs. Each switch writes its steps to the connection log (`wifi: …`).

## First start

Without `app.json` the app shows the setup screen "Connect to Home Assistant": Home Assistant instances found over mDNS (`_home-assistant._tcp`, the first one is listed) and a keypad for the address (digits, `.`, `:`). Without a port it tries 8123, then 80. It checks that Home Assistant answers (`/auth/providers` needs no login and only exists there), writes `app.json` and restarts the app, which then shows the pairing QR code.

## Configuration

`/mnt/persist/nspanel/app.json` holds the connection settings; the login token is kept separately in `ha_auth.json`.

```json
{"ha_url": "http://192.168.1.10:8123"}
```

- `ha_url`: the address of Home Assistant, with the port if it uses one (Home Assistant OS installed since 2026.8 listens on port 80; older installations and containers on 8123). Use the IP address: the panel does not resolve `.local` (mDNS) names.

- `dashboard`: url_path of the dashboard to show; empty or missing = the default dashboard. It can also be chosen on the panel's settings screen or with the Dashboard select in Home Assistant.
- `http_port` (default 8080): port of the login callback server.

## Build, run, develop

```bash
tools/build_image.sh       # builds host Rust + fontconfig, then the app (tools/build_app.sh), then the image with the app
tools/build_app.sh         # app only -> /root/nspanel/app-build/target/aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu/release/nspanel-app
GPU=1 tools/build_app.sh   # the GPU app (feature gpu) -> /root/nspanel/app-build-gpu/…; its UI bundle copy in /opt/panelos-ui/share/ui
```

- The image starts the app from `/etc/init.d/S90nspanel-app` (restarted when it exits, output to syslog).
- The GPU interface runs from two bundles; without them the image's app runs with the software renderer:
  - the web bundle (`tools/build_web.sh`, `buildroot/configs/panelos_web_defconfig`): its `rootfs.squashfs` as `/mnt/persist/panelos-web.squashfs`, mounted at `/opt/panelos-web` (`S50panelos-web`); it brings WPE WebKit and the GPU libraries (Mesa Panfrost, GBM);
  - the UI bundle (`GPU=1 tools/build_app.sh`): the app and the pictures and fonts of its pages (`bin/`, `share/`), as `/mnt/data/panelos-ui.squashfs` or `/mnt/persist/panelos-ui.squashfs`, mounted at `/opt/panelos-ui` (`S51panelos-ui`); for development a folder `/mnt/data/panelos-ui-dev` with the same layout is used instead.
- Development aids:
  - `NSPANEL_FIXTURE=<file>` renders a saved dashboard config + states without Home Assistant; the fixture may also carry `panel` (integration config), `forecast_hourly` and `forecast_daily`.
  - `NSPANEL_DEV_INPUT=/tmp/nspanel-input` creates a FIFO: lines `tap x y`, `down x y`, `move x y`, `up x y` act as touches, `swipe x1 y1 x2 y2 ms` is a swipe with a move every 16 ms, `pinch x y d1 d2 ms` two fingers one above the other round (x, y), d1 px apart, spreading to d2 px apart in `ms` (the second finger lands 40 ms after the first, a move every 16 ms, then both lift), JSON lines `{"web_load": url}`, `{"web_scroll_find": 1}` then `{"web_scroll": [px, ms]}` (a scripted scroll, no touches) and `{"web_skip": [path, ...]}` drive the web view for measurements, JSON lines are app commands (`{"show": {...}}` with the integration's fields, `{"assist": [state, heard, answer]}`, `{"page": n}`, `{"act": [what, value]}`, `{"config": {...}}` integration settings kept over Home Assistant's, `{"notes": {"kind": "added", "notes": {id: {...}}}}` a notification change as Home Assistant sends it, `{"connecting": true}`, `{"offline": n}`, `{"dark": true}`, `{"frame_stats": true}`, `{"glass_pass": false}` the rain on the glass's GPU pass off (`true` on again; `PANELOS_GLASS=0` starts without it), `{"saver": true}` the screensaver at once, `{"pages": ["kind:look", ...]}`, `{"patrol": true}`, `{"cars_test": n}`).
  - `NSPANEL_FRAME_STATS=1` logs frames per second, frame intervals and render times every 5 s, and each frame over 40 ms with its parts (recording, flush, flip wait, swap). While drops are drawn on the glass, a line `glass` every 5 s: its frames a second, the rain level, the drops, beads and streak points, and the CPU time of its simulation and of its GPU pass (p50 / p99); the pass's own GPU time is part of the frame's render time.
  - `kill -USR1 <pid>` writes the screen to `/tmp/nspanel-screen.ppm`; `kill -USR2 <pid>` scrolls one page.

## Dependencies and gotchas

- Slint 1.18: the GPU app uses the Skia renderer (`i-slint-renderer-skia`, vendored with one change, see `app/vendor/README.md`) on OpenGL ES through GBM and EGL (`gbm`, `glutin`); fonts and pictures come from the UI bundle. The image's fallback build uses `renderer-software` and `software-renderer-systemfonts` (runtime TrueType rendering, so any text from Home Assistant renders, accents included).
- `software-renderer-systemfonts` links **fontconfig**, and Slint's font backend (fontique 0.11) **panics if fontconfig finds no font at all**. The image therefore carries fontconfig and one system font (DejaVu Sans).
- Embedding pre-rendered glyphs (`EmbedForSoftwareRenderer`) was not chosen: it only embeds ASCII plus the characters of the UI's string literals, so dynamic text would miss glyphs.
- Other crates: drm, tungstenite (plain `ws://`), ureq 2 (plain HTTP), qrcode, jpeg-decoder, serde_json, minimp3. No TLS yet: an `https://` Home Assistant URL needs a TLS feature.
- The touchscreen shows as "Hynitron CST226SE Touchscreen" (kernel patch 0002 sets a per-chip input name; the mainline driver hard-codes "cst3xx").
